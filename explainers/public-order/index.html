“Law and order” or “public order”? The concentric circles test

Why not every crime justifies preventive detention under Article 22.

Preventive detention allows custody without trial, so courts insist on strict thresholds. In Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia v. State of Bihar (1966), the Supreme Court pictured three concentric circles:

  • Outer circle: law and order. Offences affecting individuals. Use ordinary prosecution.
  • Middle circle: public order. Disturbances affecting the wider community’s peace and tranquillity.
  • Inner circle: security of the State. The gravest threats.

The High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh recently reaffirmed that not every breach of law disturbs “public order” so as to justify preventive detention.

Preventive detention is for the inner circles, not for ordinary crime.
