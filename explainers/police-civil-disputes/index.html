Can the police summon you to “settle” a property dispute?

Police investigate crime; civil courts decide property disputes.

Boundary quarrels, tenancy fights and partition claims often end up at the local police station in the hope of an informal “settlement”. The law draws a firm line.

  • Limited police power. Under the CrPC, now the BNSS, police powers cover preventing and investigating offences. They do not include adjudicating civil rights.
  • Not arbitration hubs. The Karnataka High Court has said officers cannot summon citizens to the station just to broker a settlement in purely civil or property matters.
  • Civil courts decide. Title, easements, leases and eviction belong before competent civil courts, where evidence can be tested.
  • Liberty is at stake. Coercive summons in non-criminal disputes engage Article 21.
