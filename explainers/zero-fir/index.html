Can I file an FIR at any police station? Zero FIR explained

Yes. Under Section 173 of the BNSS, police must record a cognizable offence wherever it happened and transfer it. How to file, and what to do if refused.

Yes. If you report a cognizable offence, the officer in charge of any police station must record it, even if the offence took place in another area. The station registers it as a “Zero FIR” and transfers it to the police station that has jurisdiction, which then investigates.

Before July 2024 this rule came from court judgments and police circulars. Section 173(1) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), which replaced Section 154 of the CrPC, now writes it into the law: information about a cognizable offence may be given “irrespective of the area where the offence is committed”.

  • Oral or electronic. You can report in person or by electronic communication (an e-FIR). An electronic report is taken on record once you sign it within three days (BNSS s. 173(1)).
  • Free copy. You are entitled to a copy of the FIR free of cost (s. 173(2)).
  • Preliminary enquiry, in a narrow class. For offences punishable with three to less than seven years, the police may, with the permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent, hold a preliminary enquiry for up to 14 days to see whether there is a prima facie case (s. 173(3)). For other cognizable offences, registration cannot wait.
  • If the station refuses. Send the substance of your complaint in writing, by post, to the Superintendent of Police, who may investigate or direct an investigation (s. 173(4)). If that fails, you can apply to the Magistrate (s. 175(3)).
  • Registration is a duty. The Supreme Court held in Lalita Kumari v. Government of U.P. (2013) that recording an FIR is mandatory once information discloses a cognizable offence. A public servant who fails to record information in specified offences against women commits an offence under BNS s. 199.

Why it matters. Delay can cost evidence: CCTV footage is overwritten, injuries heal and witnesses scatter. A Zero FIR starts the clock at once, wherever the victim happens to be, which is why it was first urged after the 2012 Delhi gang-rape case by the Justice Verma Committee. Where a woman reports a sexual offence, the information is to be recorded by a woman police officer, and the law lays down further safeguards for recording her statement. A Zero FIR does not mean the first station will investigate; its job is to record, act on anything urgent and transfer the case without delay.

Cognizable or not? A cognizable offence is one where the police can arrest without a warrant, such as theft, cheating, assault or sexual offences. For non-cognizable offences, the police record the information and refer you to the Magistrate. Our explainer on police and civil disputes covers what the police cannot do in purely civil matters.

Practical steps. Note the date, time, place and people involved before you go. Ask for the FIR number and your free copy. If you report online, check your State police portal for the e-FIR facility and remember to sign within three days. In an emergency, call 112.
