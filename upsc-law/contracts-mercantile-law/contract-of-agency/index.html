Definition (s. 182)

An agent is a person employed to do any act for another or to represent another in dealings with third persons; the person represented is the principal. Consideration is not necessary to create an agency (s. 185). Any person of the age of majority and sound mind may employ an agent (s. 183); anyone may be an agent, but a minor agent is not responsible to the principal (s. 184).

Creation of agency

  1. Express (written or oral) (s. 187).
  2. Implied from conduct or circumstances (s. 187), including by the relationship of husband and wife for necessaries.
  3. Agency of necessity (s. 189): an agent has authority in an emergency to do all acts to protect the principal from loss, as a person of ordinary prudence would do in his own case.
  4. Ratification (ss. 196–200): the principal may ratify acts done without his knowledge or authority, with retrospective effect. Conditions: the act was done on behalf of the principal, the principal existed and was competent, ratification is of the whole transaction (s. 199), with full knowledge (s. 198), and it does not injure a third person (s. 200).
  5. Agency by estoppel / holding out (s. 237): a principal is bound when he induces a third person to believe that the agent had authority.

Authority of agents

  • Actual authority (express or implied) (ss. 186–188) and ostensible (apparent) authority.
  • Agent's authority in an emergency (s. 189).
  • Sub-agents (ss. 190–195): delegatus non potest delegare (an agent cannot delegate) except by custom or the nature of the agency; a properly appointed sub-agent binds the principal; a substituted agent (s. 194) is the principal's own agent.

Duties of the agent (ss. 211–218)

  • Follow instructions or the customs of the trade (s. 211).
  • Act with reasonable skill and diligence (s. 212).
  • Render proper accounts (s. 213); communicate with the principal (s. 214).
  • Do not deal on his own account without disclosure (ss. 215–216); no secret profits.
  • Pay sums received for the principal (s. 218).

Duties and liabilities of the principal (ss. 222–225)

  • Indemnify the agent against consequences of lawful acts (s. 222) and acts done in good faith (s. 223), but not criminal acts (s. 224).
  • Compensate for injury caused by the principal's neglect (s. 225).
  • Agent's lien over the principal's property for remuneration (s. 221).

Effect of agency on contracts with third persons

  • Contracts made by an agent bind the principal (s. 226).
  • Undisclosed principal: the third party may sue either the agent or the principal (s. 231 and ss. 232–233).
  • Personal liability of the agent (s. 230): where the agent contracts for a foreign principal, the principal is undisclosed, or the principal cannot be sued, unless otherwise agreed.
  • Misrepresentation and fraud by agents bind the principal if within authority (s. 238).

Termination (ss. 201–210)

  • By revocation by the principal, renunciation by the agent, completion of business, death or insanity of either, insolvency of the principal (s. 201).
  • Irrevocable agency: an agency coupled with the agent's interest in the subject matter cannot be terminated to the prejudice of that interest (s. 202).
  • Notice requirements and compensation for premature revocation (ss. 205–206).
  • Termination of the agent's authority takes effect, as regards third persons, when it becomes known to them (s. 208).

Revision points

  • s. 182; no consideration needed (s. 185).
  • Modes: express, implied, necessity (s. 189), ratification (ss. 196–200), estoppel (s. 237).
  • Sub-agent vs substituted agent (ss. 190–195).
  • Undisclosed principal (s. 231); agent's personal liability (s. 230); irrevocable agency (s. 202).
Syllabus unit 8 · Asked 9 times · 2014–2026

Answer writing: Contract of agency

Decode the demand. Questions test the nature of agency (representative character, derivative authority), kinds of authority and liability.

Open with

An agent does not merely act for another; an agent represents the principal and can change the principal's legal relations.

Answer frame

  1. Definition and essentials
  2. Agent vs servant vs independent contractor
  3. Kinds of authority (actual, apparent, necessity)
  4. Ratification
  5. Termination and irrevocable agency

Concept in brief

An agent is employed to act for, or represent, a principal in dealings with third persons (s. 182). What marks an agent is representative character and derivative authority: the power to bind the principal. Agency can arise by express or implied authority, ratification (s. 196), estoppel/holding out (s. 237) or necessity; it is terminated under s. 201, unless coupled with interest (s. 202).

Key provisions

  • ss. 182–238

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Lakshminarayan Ram Gopal v. Govt. of Hyderabad (1954): Agent distinguished from servant.
  • Freeman & Lockyer v. Buckhurst Park Properties (1964): Apparent authority.
  • Watteau v. Fenwick (1893): Usual authority of undisclosed agent.
  • Keighley, Maxsted & Co. v. Durant (1901): Ratification only by a disclosed principal.
  • Great Northern Railway v. Swaffield (1874): Agency of necessity.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Platform economy: are gig workers agents?
  • Real-estate agents under RERA

Critical lens

Apparent authority protects third parties but can bind principals unfairly.

Close with

Agency law is about trust and authority, which is why derivative authority is its defining mark.

Previous-year questions (9)
  1. 2026“Every person who acts for another is not an agent. Representative character and derivative authority may briefly be said to be the distinguishing features of an agent.” In view of the above, discuss the nature of agency. [P-II 8(c), 15 marks]
    How to approach: Discuss the nature of agency: representative character and derivative authority; agent vs servant (Lakshminarayan Ram Gopal) vs independent contractor; kinds of authority; ratification.
  2. 2024Mutual rights and duties of the Principal and agent may be wholly provided for in their contract. Discuss the general duties of the agent with special reference to the duty of reasonable care and skill.
  3. 2023"The doctrine of 'Undisclosed Principal' comes into play when the agent neither disclosed the existence of his principal nor his representative character." In such cases, discuss the rights and liabilities of the Principals, the agent and the third parties.
  4. 2022What are the essentials of an agency? How is an agency created and terminated under the Indian Contract Act, 1872?
  5. 2021In legal phraseology, "every person who acts for another is not an agent". Comment.
  6. 2019"Contract of agency is revocable like an ordinary contract, but sometimes it is impossible to repudiate it." Analyse with the help of decided cases and relevant provisions.
  7. 2018"If a person falsely represents that he is an agent of another, the principal may ratify the act even though the same was done without his authority." Discuss, in the light of the above statement, the essentials of valid ratification and its effect.
  8. 2016"The revocation of agent's authority can be made by the principal subject to certain rules." Examine these rules in the light of protection to agent.
  9. 2014"The liability of sub-agent towards principal is not direct, except in case of fraud and wilful wrong." Explain, giving reasons.
