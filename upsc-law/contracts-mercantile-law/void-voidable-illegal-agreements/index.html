Void, voidable and illegal agreements

Study notes

Key definitionsUnlawful consideration and object (s. 23)Agreements expressly declared voidVoid vs illegalRestitution (s. 65)

Key definitions

  • Void agreement (s. 2(g)): not enforceable by law from the start.
  • Voidable contract (s. 2(i)): enforceable at the option of one party (consent not free).
  • Contract that becomes void (s. 2(j)): enforceable when made, later unenforceable (impossibility).
  • Unenforceable: valid but cannot be sued on for a technical reason (unstamped, time-barred, unregistered).
  • Illegal: forbidden by law; collateral agreements are also tainted.

Unlawful consideration and object (s. 23)

The consideration or object is unlawful if it is:

  1. forbidden by law
  2. of such a nature that it would defeat the provisions of any law
  3. fraudulent
  4. involves or implies injury to the person or property of another
  5. regarded by the court as immoral or opposed to public policy
  6. Gherulal Parakh v. Mahadeodas Maiya (1959): public policy must be applied cautiously; courts should not create new heads but may apply recognised ones (trading with an enemy, interference with justice, marriage brokerage). Wagering agreements are void but not illegal, so collateral transactions are valid.
  7. Central Inland Water Transport Corp. v. Brojo Nath Ganguly (1986): a term allowing the employer to terminate permanent employees on three months' notice was unconscionable and void under s. 23, where parties had unequal bargaining power.
  8. Agreements to pay bribes, sell public offices, or stifle prosecution of non-compoundable offences are void.

Agreements expressly declared void

  • s. 24: consideration or object unlawful in part.
  • s. 25: without consideration (subject to exceptions).
  • s. 26: in restraint of marriage (except of minors).
  • s. 27: in restraint of trade: void except on sale of goodwill. Indian law is stricter than English law; even partial restraints are void. - Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning (1967): a negative covenant not to work for others during the term of employment is valid. - Superintendence Co. of India v. Krishan Murgai (1980): a post-employment restraint is void. - Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan (2006): a "right of first refusal" after the contract ended was void.
  • s. 28: in restraint of legal proceedings; clauses that limit the time for enforcing rights are void (1997 amendment), but arbitration clauses are saved; exclusive jurisdiction clauses choosing one of several competent courts are valid (A.B.C. Laminart v. A.P. Agencies, 1989).
  • s. 29: uncertain agreements.
  • s. 30: wagering agreements (except horse-race prizes of ₹500 or more). Statutes may regulate games of skill.
  • s. 36: contingent on impossible events; s. 56: impossible acts.

Void vs illegal

VoidIllegal
ScopeNarrowerWider (every illegal agreement is void)
Collateral transactionsValidAlso void
RestitutionPossible (s. 65)Generally no (in pari delicto)

Restitution (s. 65)

When an agreement is discovered to be void or becomes void, any person who has received an advantage must restore it. It does not apply where the parties knew the agreement was void from the start, or to minors (Mohori Bibee).

Revision points

  • ss. 2(g), (i), (j); void vs voidable vs illegal vs unenforceable.
  • s. 23 heads; Gherulal (1959); Brojo Nath (1986) unconscionability.
  • s. 27: Golikari (valid during employment) vs Krishan Murgai (post-employment void).
  • s. 28 and exclusive jurisdiction (A.B.C. Laminart); s. 30 wagering.
Syllabus unit 3 · Asked 13 times · 2014–2025

Answer writing: Void, voidable, illegal and unenforceable agreements

Decode the demand. Questions test void vs voidable vs illegal, minors' agreements, restraint of trade and wagering.

Open with

Not every agreement is enforceable; the Act declares some void for policy reasons.

Answer frame

  1. Classification
  2. Minors' agreements (Mohori Bibee)
  3. Unlawful objects and public policy (s. 23)
  4. Restraints (ss. 26–28)
  5. Consequences (s. 65)

Concept in brief

Void agreements include those with minors, with unlawful object or consideration, without consideration (save s. 25 exceptions), in restraint of marriage, trade or legal proceedings, uncertain and wagering agreements. Voidable contracts can be avoided by the party whose consent was not free. Illegal agreements are void and taint collateral transactions.

Key provisions

  • ss. 11, 23–30, 65
  • Specific Relief Act, 1963, s. 33

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose (1903, PC): Minor's agreement void ab initio.
  • Gherulal Parakh v. Mahadeodas Maiya (1959): Public policy narrowly applied; wagering not illegal.
  • Nordenfelt v. Maxim Nordenfelt (1894): Reasonable restraints (English law).
  • Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning (1967): Restraint during employment valid.
  • Superintendence Co. of India v. Krishan Murgai (1981): Post-employment restraint void.
  • Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan (2006): Right-of-first-refusal clause void under s. 27.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Non-compete clauses in the employment and start-up market
  • Online gaming and wagering debates

Critical lens

The absolute bar in s. 27 is stricter than English law and resists modern commercial practice.

Close with

Public policy limits keep freedom of contract consistent with public interest.

Previous-year questions (13)

Agreements in Restraint of Trade, Marriage, or Legal Proceedings

  1. 2025"Every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind is to that extent void." Discuss the statement along with the circumstances in which such agreements have been considered valid by the courts.
  2. 2018Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, makes agreements in restraint of legal proceedings void. Are there any exceptions to this rule? Discuss with the help of relevant provisions and decided cases.
  3. 2015"Every agreement by which any party thereto is restricted absolutely from enforcing his rights under or in respect of any contract, by the usual legal proceedings in the ordinary Tribunals, or which limits the time within which he may thus enforce his right is void to that extent." Explain the exceptions, if any, of this principle.
  4. 2014"Right not to compete" is a right meant for promoting business through small groups called firms. Explain.

Agreements against Public Policy / Illegal Agreements

  1. 2024'An illegal contract is always void, but a void contract is not always illegal.' Examine while illustrating both types of contract.
  2. 2020"No court will lend its aid to a man who found his cause of action upon an immoral or illegal act." Are there any exceptions to the above-said rule? Explain.
  3. 2017"Public policy is like an 'unruly horse' which cannot be controlled easily." Explain the statement and mention the agreements which are against public policy.

Minor's Agreements

  1. 2023"A minor's contract being void, ordinarily it should be wholly devoid of all effects. If there is no contract, there should, indeed, be no contractual obligation on either side." Explain with case laws.
  2. 2021Minor's contract is 'void ab initio'. Comment.
  3. 2017"Minority can only be claimed as a shield but not as a sword." Explain the statement and mention the situations when a minor is liable under the law of contract.
  4. 2015Explain the law relating to minors’ agreements.

Agreements without Consideration (and its exceptions)

  1. 2022"An agreement without consideration is void." Is there any exception to it? Discuss by giving suitable illustrations.
  2. 2016A owed B ₹ 1,000, but the debt is barred by the Limitation Act, 1963. Subsequently, A signs a written promise to pay ₹ 1,000 on account of the previous debt. Decide the validity of this agreement.
