Theories of punishment

  • Deterrent: punish to discourage the offender (specific) and society (general). Bentham.
  • Retributive: punishment as deserved ("just deserts"); proportionality to the wrong. Kant.
  • Preventive: incapacitate the offender (imprisonment, preventive detention).
  • Reformative: rehabilitate (probation, parole, open prisons, community service).
  • Expiatory: atonement.

Indian courts combine these; sentencing must be proportionate (State of M.P. v. Bablu Natt, 2009 and others).

Kinds of punishment (BNS s. 4; IPC s. 53)

  1. Death
  2. Imprisonment for life (for the remainder of natural life, Gopal Vinayak Godse v. State of Maharashtra, 1961; Union of India v. V. Sriharan, 2015)
  3. Imprisonment: rigorous or simple
  4. Forfeiture of property
  5. Fine
  6. Community service (new in the BNS, for petty offences such as small thefts, defamation, public intoxication and failure to appear)

Also: solitary confinement (BNS s. 11), and probation under the Probation of Offenders Act, 1958 and BNSS s. 401.

Capital punishment: the constitutional journey

  • Jagmohan Singh v. State of U.P. (1973): the death penalty is constitutional; judicial discretion is guided by the law.
  • CrPC 1973, s. 354(3): "special reasons" required for death; life imprisonment the rule (now BNSS s. 393(3)).
  • Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980, 5 judges): upheld the death penalty but limited it to the "rarest of rare" cases where life imprisonment is "unquestionably foreclosed"; weigh aggravating and mitigating circumstances of the crime and the criminal.
  • Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab (1983): categories (manner, motive, anti-social nature, magnitude, victim's personality) and a balance sheet of circumstances.
  • Mithu v. State of Punjab (1983): mandatory death under IPC s. 303 struck down.
  • Shatrughan Chauhan v. Union of India (2014): delay in deciding mercy petitions, mental illness and solitary confinement are grounds to commute.
  • Mohd. Arif v. Registrar, Supreme Court (2014): review petitions in death cases to be heard in open court.
  • Manoj v. State of M.P. (2022): courts must collect mitigation material (psychological assessment, jail conduct, background) before sentencing; the Court later referred the question of uniform sentencing hearings to a Constitution Bench.
  • Shabnam v. Union of India and executions (Nirbhaya convicts, 2020) show the finality issues.

The abolition debate

  • For retention: deterrence, retribution for the worst crimes, public sentiment, terrorism.
  • For abolition: no proven deterrent effect, risk of wrongful execution, arbitrariness in application (the "rarest of rare" test is applied inconsistently, as the Court itself admitted in Santosh Bariyar, 2009), disproportionate impact on the poor.
  • Law Commission, 262nd Report (2015): abolish except for terrorism-related offences and waging war.
  • International: Art. 6 ICCPR allows death only for the most serious crimes; the Second Optional Protocol (abolition) has not been ratified by India; over two-thirds of countries are abolitionist in law or practice.
  • The BNS retains death for murder, some forms of rape, organised crime causing death and terrorist acts, and adds it for mob lynching (s. 103(2)).

Revision points

  • Theories: deterrent, retributive, preventive, reformative.
  • BNS s. 4 adds community service.
  • Jagmohan (1973) → Bachan Singh (1980) → Machhi Singh (1983) → Mithu (1983) → Shatrughan Chauhan (2014) → Manoj (2022).
  • Law Commission 262nd Report (2015).
Syllabus unit 2 · Asked 5 times · 2013–2026

Answer writing: Kinds of punishment and emerging trends as to the abolition of capital punishment

Decode the demand. Questions test the theories of punishment, the death-penalty debate and new forms such as community service. Pair penology with case law.

Open with

Punishment in a constitutional democracy must serve reform and social protection, not only retribution.

Answer frame

  1. Theories: deterrent, retributive, preventive, reformative, restorative
  2. BNS s. 4 and community service
  3. Death penalty: Bachan Singh to Manoj
  4. Sentencing policy and its absence
  5. Way forward

Concept in brief

BNS s. 4 lists punishments: death, imprisonment for life, rigorous or simple imprisonment, forfeiture, fine and, newly, community service. Community service is unpaid work that benefits the community (BNSS s. 23 explanation) and is prescribed for petty offences such as first-time theft below ₹5,000 with restitution, so that local harms can be repaired locally. Death is reserved for the 'rarest of rare' cases after weighing mitigating circumstances.

Key provisions

  • IPC s. 53 → BNS s. 4
  • BNS ss. 202, 209, 226, 303(2) proviso, 355, 356(2) (community service)
  • BNSS s. 23
  • Probation of Offenders Act, 1958

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980): Death penalty valid; 'rarest of rare' doctrine.
  • Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab (1983): Categories for 'rarest of rare'.
  • Mithu v. State of Punjab (1983): Mandatory death sentence (s. 303 IPC) unconstitutional.
  • Shatrughan Chauhan v. Union of India (2014): Inordinate delay in mercy petitions can justify commutation.
  • Swamy Shraddananda v. State of Karnataka (2008): Fixed-term life sentence beyond 14 years as an alternative to death.
  • Manoj v. State of M.P. (2022): Mitigation investigation required before death sentence.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Law Commission 35th (1967) and 262nd (2015) Reports on the death penalty
  • Restorative justice and the JJ Act, 2015 (community service for children)
  • Comparative: UK community service orders
  • Data: Project 39A's annual death-penalty statistics

Critical lens

Community service can repair local harm (cleaning public spaces, helping local bodies) if courts define it clearly and supervise it.

Close with

A graded, reform-oriented sentencing policy is the missing link in Indian penology.

Previous-year questions (5)
  1. 2026How can ‘community service’, as a type of punishment, be used to address local issues? Discuss. [P-II 1(b), 10 marks]
    How to approach: Discuss. BNS s. 4(f) and BNSS s. 23; offences carrying it; uses for local issues (cleaning, traffic help, civic work); safeguards (consent, supervision, dignity). Edge: JJ Act and UK orders.
  2. 2025Under what circumstances can the appropriate government commute the sentence of death and life imprisonment for any other punishment? Discuss. [3(e)]
  3. 2020Though the 'capital punishment' is not abolished in India, the recent trends show that the Supreme Court, in appeals, is inclined to modify the same and sentence the convict to life imprisonment with further direction that the convict must not be released from prison before he/she actually serves certain specified number of years usually 20, 25 or 30 years. Do you think the Court is justified in issuing such directions? Comment. [3(a)]
  4. 2015"The criminal justice is administered in India by punishing the wrongdoer, and the object of punishment is viewed differently by different criminologists." Elucidate. [1(b)]
  5. 2013“Fake police encounters are nothing but murders and police officers committing it without any protection of law to them deserve death penalty as the cases fall into rarer of the rarest category.” Comment. [1(b)]
