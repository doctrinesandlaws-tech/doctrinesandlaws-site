Background

The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (PCA) consolidated the 1947 Act and IPC ss. 161–165A. The 2018 amendment (in force 26 July 2018) aligned it with the UN Convention against Corruption (ratified by India in 2011).

Key definitions (s. 2)

  • Public servant (s. 2(c)): wide; includes government employees, judges, persons in public sector corporations, university teachers, office-bearers of co-operative societies receiving government aid, and anyone performing a public duty. P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State (1998): MPs are public servants. CBI v. Ramesh Gelli (2016): private bank officials are public servants by s. 46A of the Banking Regulation Act.
  • Undue advantage (s. 2(d)): any gratification other than legal remuneration.

Offences after 2018

  • s. 7: a public servant obtaining, accepting or attempting to obtain undue advantage to perform a public duty improperly or dishonestly, or forbearing; 3 to 7 years.
  • s. 7A: taking undue advantage to influence a public servant by corrupt or illegal means or personal influence.
  • s. 8: giving or promising undue advantage is now an offence (bribe-giving), except where the person was compelled and reports within seven days.
  • s. 9: offence by a commercial organisation whose associated person gives a bribe; defence of "adequate procedures" (compliance programme).
  • s. 10: persons in charge of a commercial organisation are liable if the offence was committed with their consent or connivance.
  • s. 11: public servant obtaining undue advantage without consideration from a person concerned in proceedings.
  • s. 13: criminal misconduct narrowed to (a) fraudulent misappropriation of property entrusted, and (b) illicit enrichment (assets disproportionate to known sources of income); 4 to 10 years.

Procedure and safeguards

  • s. 17A (2018): no inquiry or investigation into decisions taken in the discharge of official functions without prior approval of the competent authority, except when caught red-handed. (Earlier, s. 6A of the DSPE Act was struck down in Subramanian Swamy v. Director, CBI (2014), which CBI v. R.R. Kishore (2023) held operates retrospectively.)
  • s. 19: prior sanction for prosecution; decision within three months (extendable by one month). Subramanian Swamy v. Manmohan Singh (2012): a citizen may seek sanction; delay defeats the law.
  • s. 20: presumption once acceptance of undue advantage is proved.
  • s. 4: special judges; trial to be completed within two years (extendable).
  • Attachment and forfeiture of property (ss. 18A, via the Criminal Law Amendment Ordinance, 1944).

Leading cases

  • Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998): independence of CBI and CVC; led to the CVC Act, 2003.
  • Neeraj Dutta v. State (Govt. of NCT of Delhi) (2022, 5 judges): demand and acceptance of a bribe can be proved by circumstantial evidence when direct evidence is unavailable (for example, if the complainant dies or turns hostile).
  • Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024): legislators have no immunity under Arts. 105/194 for bribery.
  • State of M.P. v. Ram Singh (2000): corruption is a "serious threat" to society; courts should interpret the Act purposively.

Assessment

  • Strengths: bribe-giving and corporate liability now covered; illicit enrichment; time limits.
  • Concerns: s. 17A and s. 19 can shield officials; delays in sanction; low conviction rates; the need for whistle-blower protection and independent investigation.

Revision points

  • 2018 amendment: ss. 7, 7A, 8 (bribe-giving), 9–10 (corporate), 13 (narrowed).
  • s. 17A prior approval; s. 19 sanction; s. 20 presumption.
  • Neeraj Dutta (2022) circumstantial proof; Vineet Narain (1998); Sita Soren (2024).
Syllabus unit 10 · Asked 9 times · 2014–2026

Answer writing: Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988

Decode the demand. Questions test demand and acceptance, the 2018 amendments and fact problems. Apply s. 7, presumption under s. 20, and sanction.

Open with

Corruption is a breach of public trust; the PCA, 1988 as amended in 2018 punishes both taker and giver.

Answer frame

  1. Key definitions (public servant, undue advantage)
  2. Offences: ss. 7, 7A, 8, 13
  3. Procedure: s. 17A approval, s. 19 sanction
  4. Proof: demand and acceptance (Neeraj Dutta)
  5. Application and conclusion

Concept in brief

The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, amended in 2018, punishes a public servant who obtains, accepts or attempts to obtain an undue advantage to perform a public duty improperly (s. 7), and also the bribe-giver (s. 8). A demand for money to process a routine application on time is itself an offence under s. 7. Investigation of decisions taken in official functions needs prior approval (s. 17A, except when caught red-handed), and prosecution needs sanction (s. 19).

Key provisions

  • PCA ss. 2(c), 7, 7A, 8, 9, 10, 13, 17A, 19, 20 (presumption)

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Neeraj Dutta v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2022): Demand and acceptance can be proved by circumstantial evidence.
  • B. Jayaraj v. State of A.P. (2014): Proof of demand is essential.
  • P. Satyanarayana Murthy v. DIG of Police (2015): Mere recovery of money is insufficient.
  • Subramanian Swamy v. Manmohan Singh (2012): Sanction decisions must be time-bound.
  • Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024): Legislators' bribery not protected by privilege.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Santhanam Committee (1964) and the CVC
  • UN Convention against Corruption (ratified by India in 2011)
  • 2nd ARC 4th Report on ethics in governance

Critical lens

s. 17A protects honest decisions but can delay investigation.

Close with

Swift, transparent prosecution and citizen-friendly services (time-bound delivery) together reduce petty bribery.

Previous-year questions (9)
  1. 2026‘X’, a public servant, asks ‘Y’, a person to give him an amount of Rupees ten thousand in order to process ‘Y’s routine application for a cooking gas connection on time. What offence, if any, has ‘X’ committed? Explain in the light of the relevant statutory provisions. Refer to leading cases. [P-II 4(c), 15 marks]
    How to approach: Problem. Issue: offence under PCA s. 7 (as amended 2018): demanding an undue advantage to perform a public duty is itself an offence. Rule: s. 7, presumption under s. 20, sanction under s. 19. Cases: Neeraj Dutta, B. Jayaraj. Conclude: X is liable even if no money passed.
  2. 2025"The definition of 'public servant' as per the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 is only illustrative and not exhaustive." Comment. [1(b)]
  3. 2024How far do you agree that the Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 is an important legal instrument in curbing corruption in society? Discuss. What are the types of offences recognised under this law, and what are the punishments? [2(c)]
  4. 2023Examine the term 'Undue-Advantage' as defined under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. Also discuss the persons authorised and the procedure required to be followed while investigating cases that are registered under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. [4(b)]
  5. 2020Is it mandatory to obtain a previous sanction for prosecuting a public servant for offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988? Discuss in the light of relevant provisions and the decisions of the Supreme Court. [1(c)]
  6. 2018Corruption by public servants has become a gigantic problem. Large-scale corruption retards the nation-building activities, and everyone has to suffer on their count. The efficiency of a public servant would improve only when the public servant does their duty truthfully and honestly. Therefore, in such cases, it is difficult to accept any plea of leniency in sentence (State of MP vs. Shambhu Dayal Nagar (2006) 8 SCC 693). Comment. [3(c)]
  7. 2016"The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, prevents public servants from misuse and abuse of their official capacity." Comment. [1(b)]
  8. 2015Explain the various circumstances under which a 'public servant' is said to commit the offence of "Criminal misconduct" under 'The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988'. [2(b)]
  9. 2014"Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 is an important legislation to safeguard democracy in India." Discuss. [1(e)]
