Offences against the State

  • Waging war against the Government of India (IPC s. 121 → BNS s. 147): death or life imprisonment. State (NCT of Delhi) v. Navjot Sandhu (2005): the Parliament attack amounted to waging war; the intention to strike at the sovereign authority is key.
  • Conspiracy to wage war (IPC s. 121A → BNS s. 148); collecting arms (IPC s. 122 → BNS s. 149).
  • Sedition (IPC s. 124A): Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962) read it down to speech inciting violence or public disorder; S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India (2022) kept it in abeyance. The BNS does not contain sedition, but BNS s. 152 punishes acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India (exciting secession, armed rebellion, subversive activities, separatism). Critics argue s. 152 is broader; supporters say it targets acts against the nation rather than criticism of government.

Offences against the human body

Culpable homicide and murder (IPC ss. 299–304 → BNS ss. 100–105)

  • Culpable homicide (BNS s. 100): causing death with intention of causing death, or of causing bodily injury likely to cause death, or with knowledge that the act is likely to cause death.
  • Murder (BNS s. 101): culpable homicide becomes murder in four situations (intention to cause death; injury known to be likely to cause death of that person; injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death; imminently dangerous act without excuse), subject to five exceptions (grave and sudden provocation, exceeding private defence, public servant acting in good faith, sudden fight, consent).
  • Reg v. Govinda (1876, Melvill J.) and State of A.P. v. Rayavarapu Punnayya (1976): a three-step approach comparing the degrees of intention and knowledge.
  • Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab (1958): test for "thirdly": the injury intended was present and was sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death.
  • K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra (1961): grave and sudden provocation must be judged by a reasonable person of the same class; a cooling-off period defeats the exception.
  • Punishment: murder, BNS s. 103 (death or life); group murder on grounds of identity, s. 103(2); culpable homicide not amounting to murder, s. 105; death by negligence, s. 106 (up to five years; up to two years for a registered medical practitioner during a medical procedure; hit-and-run without reporting attracts up to ten years, s. 106(2)).
  • Hurt and grievous hurt: BNS ss. 114–117; acid attack, ss. 124.

Offences against women

  • Rape (IPC s. 375 → BNS s. 63, punishment s. 64; gang rape s. 70). The 2013 amendment (Justice Verma Committee) widened the definition. Independent Thought v. Union of India (2017) read the marital exception to apply only where the wife is 18 or older; challenges to the marital exception remain pending.
  • Sexual intercourse by deceitful means (BNS s. 69): including a false promise to marry or employment.
  • Assault to outrage modesty (IPC s. 354 → BNS s. 74); sexual harassment (s. 75); voyeurism (s. 77); stalking (s. 78).
  • Dowry death (IPC s. 304B → BNS s. 80): death within seven years of marriage with cruelty or harassment "soon before death"; presumption under BSA s. 118 (IEA s. 113B). Satbir Singh v. State of Haryana (2021) explained "soon before".
  • Cruelty by husband or relatives (IPC s. 498A → BNS ss. 85–86). Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014): no automatic arrest; Social Action Forum for Manav Adhikar v. Union of India (2018) set aside the family welfare committees of Rajesh Sharma (2017).
  • Special laws: POCSO Act, 2012; Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005; POSH Act, 2013.

  • Culpable homicide vs murder: Govinda, Punnayya, Virsa Singh, Nanavati.
  • Women: BNS 63–80; Independent Thought (2017); Arnesh Kumar (2014).

Syllabus unit 8 · Asked 41 times · 2013–2026

Answer writing: Offences against the State, public tranquillity, human body, property, and women

Decode the demand. The largest Paper II unit. Expect BNS changes (sedition to s. 152, theft, sexual offences), the murder vs culpable homicide distinction, and fact problems. Use IPC → BNS mapping in every answer.

Open with

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 kept the IPC's architecture but redrew key offences, including sedition, sexual offences and organised crime.

Answer frame

  1. Identify the offence and ingredients (IPC → BNS section)
  2. Exceptions or aggravating factors
  3. Leading cases
  4. Change introduced by the BNS
  5. Application and conclusion (for problem questions)

Concept in brief

BNS reorganised specific offences. Sedition (s. 124A IPC) is replaced by s. 152 BNS, 'acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India', which drops 'disaffection towards Government' but covers secession, armed rebellion and subversive activity. Culpable homicide becomes murder unless an exception applies; Exception 4 covers a sudden fight without premeditation. Theft protects possession, not ownership. New offences include organised crime, terrorist act, mob lynching, snatching and sexual intercourse by deceitful means.

Key provisions

  • Sedition IPC 124A → BNS 152; waging war 121 → 147; promoting enmity 153A → 196
  • Unlawful assembly 141 → 189; rioting 146–147 → 191
  • Culpable homicide 299 → 100; murder 300 → 101; 302 → 103; 304 → 105; 304A → 106; dowry death 304B → 80
  • Hurt 319–325 → 114–118; kidnapping 359–361, 363 → 137; abduction 362 → 138; 366 → 87
  • Theft 378 → 303; snatching (new) 304; extortion 383 → 308; robbery 390 → 309; dacoity 391 → 310; CBT 405 → 316; cheating 415/420 → 318
  • Rape 375–376 → 63–64; gang rape 376D → 70; deceitful means/false promise (new) 69; 354 → 74; stalking 354D → 78; cruelty 498A → 85–86
  • Organised crime BNS 111; terrorist act 113; mob lynching 103(2)

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962): Sedition limited to incitement to violence or public disorder.
  • S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India (2022): S. 124A kept in abeyance.
  • Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab (1958): Test for s. 300 'thirdly'.
  • State of A.P. v. Rayavarapu Punnayya (1976): Culpable homicide vs murder.
  • K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra (1962): Grave and sudden provocation; cooling time.
  • Surinder Kumar v. Union Territory, Chandigarh (1989): Exception 4: sudden fight, no undue advantage.
  • Pyare Lal Bhargava v. State of Rajasthan (1963): Temporary removal is enough for theft.
  • Tukaram v. State of Maharashtra (1979): Mathura case; led to the 1983 amendment.
  • Deepak Gulati v. State of Haryana (2013): False promise vs breach of promise to marry.
  • Pramod Suryabhan Pawar v. State of Maharashtra (2019): Promise must be false from the start to vitiate consent.
  • Independent Thought v. Union of India (2017): Marital-rape exception read down for wives under 18.
  • Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014): No automatic arrest in s. 498A cases.
  • Kans Raj v. State of Punjab (2000): 'Soon before death' in dowry death.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Law Commission 279th Report (2023) on sedition
  • Justice Verma Committee (2013) on sexual offences
  • NCRB 'Crime in India' data on offences against women
  • Murder vs culpable homicide table (Reg v Govinda)

Draw this

Culpable homicide (s. 100) → murder (s. 101) unless Exceptions 1–5 (e.g. sudden fight) → s. 105

Critical lens

New offences such as s. 152 avoid the word 'sedition' but use broad terms that need narrow judicial reading.

Close with

Reform of the code succeeds only if courts read new offences in the light of Kedar Nath-style limits and constitutional freedoms.

Previous-year questions (41)

Offences against the State

  1. 2026From ‘sedition’ under the Indian Penal Code, 1860 to ‘acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India’ under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, how has the law changed? Explain. [P-II 1(e), 10 marks]
    How to approach: Compare s. 124A IPC with s. 152 BNS in a table: words used, conduct covered (secession, armed rebellion, subversive activities), electronic communication, punishment. Kedar Nath, Vombatkere, Law Commission 279th Report. Note concerns of vagueness.
  2. 2016"Section 124A of the IPC dealing with sedition is ultra-vires of the Constitution insofar as it seeks to punish merely bad feelings against the Government..." Comment.

Offences against the Human Body

  1. 2026How effectively does the law distinguish between ‘pre-meditated killing’ and ‘murder as a consequence of a sudden fight’? Explain in the light of relevant statutory provisions. [P-II 3(a), 20 marks]
    How to approach: Explain with statute. s. 101 BNS clauses vs Exception 4 (sudden fight, heat of passion, no undue advantage, no cruelty); premeditation points to murder; punishment under s. 103 vs s. 105. Cases: Surinder Kumar. Table of differences.

  3. 2024'It is the degree of negligence which really determines whether a particular action will amount to a rash and negligent act as required to hold a person guilty of homicide under Section 304-A of the Indian Penal Code, 1860.' Discuss.
  4. 2023Discuss the doctrine of 'Transferred Malice' as applied to law relating to culpable homicide under the Indian Penal Code, 1860.
  5. 2022Summarise the law relating to 'attempt to suicide' in India. How far has the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017, added new dimensions to the law of attempt to suicide in India?
  6. 2021Discuss 'Grave and Sudden Provocation' as a defence to the charge of murder under IPC, 1860?

  9. 2018"Section 300 (4) of the Indian Penal Code will be applicable in cases where the knowledge of the offender as to the probability of death of a person approximates to practical certainty." Illustrate the above statement.

Offences against Women

  1. 2026‘X’, a 25-year-old boy, makes a promise to ‘Y’, an 18-year-old girl, that he will marry her if she has ‘carnal-relations’ with him. The girl is reluctant, but agrees after repeated assurances from the boy that he will marry her. Fifteen days after the act, the boy sends her a text message stating that he cannot keep his promise because marriage with her does not align with his career plans and that he needs to marry an NRI girl to facilitate his settlement abroad. What offence, if any, has been committed by the boy? Discuss. In this context, explain the provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita in the light of amendments relating to sexual offences, which recently took place. [P-II 4(a), 20 marks]
    How to approach: Problem: Issue → Rule → Application → Conclusion. Both adults. Issues: rape under s. 63 with consent vitiated by misconception (BNS s. 28) vs the new s. 69 (sexual intercourse by deceitful means or false promise to marry). Test: was the promise false from inception (Pramod Suryabhan Pawar, Deepak Gulati)? Breakup 15 days later suggests intention; conclude on s. 69 with reasoning.
  2. 2025Critically examine the provisions relating to a few major offences which fall under the 'offences against marriage' in the criminal law of India. Support your answer with case law.

  8. 2021Differentiate between the following: (i) 'Kidnapping' and 'Abduction'...

  10. 2020If a woman watches or captures the image of another woman engaging in a private act..., does that amount to an offence of 'voyeurism' under the Indian Penal Code? Discuss.

Offences against Property

  1. 2026“Theft is an offence against possession, not ownership.” Comment. Also explain the changes introduced in the law relating to theft under the provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. [P-II 4(b), 15 marks]
    How to approach: Comment + changes. Ingredients of theft; possession as the focus (owner can steal own pawned property, Illustration (j)); BNS changes: s. 303(2) graded punishment, community service for first-time small thefts with restitution, new snatching offence (s. 304).
  2. 2025"Dacoity is an aggravated form of theft and robbery." Explain with relevant provisions and case laws.
  3. 2024"It is the mode of acquiring possession of property of other party with/without his consent, which determines the type of offence against property and thus distinguishes theft, misappropriation and Criminal breach of trust." Discuss.
  4. 2023"Dishonest Intention is the gist of the offence of Theft." Examine the above statement with the help of relevant illustrations. Also discuss how 'theft' is different from 'dishonest misappropriation of property.'
  5. 2021Differentiate between the following: ... (iii) 'Criminal Breach of Trust' and 'Dishonest Misappropriation of property'.
  6. 2020Unless the ingredients of either 'theft' or 'extortion' are present, neither the offence of 'robbery' nor the offence of 'dacoity' can be made out. Explain.
  7. 2018Six people decided to carry out a dacoity of a bank in a village... one of the dacoits (X) killed Mr. Y, who tried to obstruct his way. Decide liability for the murder committed by one of them in view of Sections 391 and 396 of the Indian Penal Code.
  8. 2017In all robberies, there is either theft or extortion. Explain.
  9. 2015Whether the conduct of depriving someone from possession of property without causing wrongful gain or wrongful loss amount to theft? Comment.
  10. 2014"Criminal breach of trust and cheating are two distinct offences generally involving dishonest intention but mutually exclusive and different in basic concept." Explain with the help of decided cases.
  11. 2013The natural calamity in Uttarakhand left hundreds dead. Some people were seen removing gold ornaments, watches and other valuables from these dead bodies... Under which provision of the Indian Penal Code can they be prosecuted? Discuss.
