New International Economic Order (NIEO)

  • Newly independent States sought fairer terms in trade, finance and technology.
  • GA Resolution 1803 (1962): permanent sovereignty over natural resources; nationalisation with "appropriate compensation".
  • GA Resolutions 3201 and 3202 (1974): Declaration and Programme of Action on the NIEO.
  • Charter of Economic Rights and Duties of States (Resolution 3281, 1974): each State may regulate foreign investment and nationalise, with compensation determined by its own law.
  • The NIEO did not become binding law, but it shaped special and differential treatment for developing countries and the right to development (Declaration on the Right to Development, 1986).

The Bretton Woods institutions (1944)

  • International Monetary Fund (IMF): monetary cooperation, exchange stability and balance-of-payments support; Special Drawing Rights (SDRs, 1969). Lending with conditionality; quota-based voting. India's 1991 crisis led to an IMF programme and liberalisation.
  • World Bank Group: IBRD (loans to middle-income countries), IDA (concessional credit), IFC, MIGA and ICSID (investment disputes).
  • Criticism: weighted voting that favours developed countries; conditionality; slow quota reform (2010 reform took effect only in 2016).

From GATT to the WTO

  • GATT 1947: tariff reductions through rounds; key principles: - Most-favoured-nation treatment (Art. I) - National treatment (Art. III) - Ban on quantitative restrictions (Art. XI) - General exceptions (Art. XX: public morals, health, conservation) and security exceptions (Art. XXI)
  • WTO (Marrakesh, 1995): covers goods (GATT 1994), services (GATS), intellectual property (TRIPS), agriculture, SPS and TBT.
  • Dispute Settlement Understanding: panels, the Appellate Body, binding rulings and authorised retaliation. The Appellate Body has been non-functional since December 2019 because appointments were blocked; some members use the interim appeal arrangement (MPIA). India is not part of the MPIA.
  • Special and differential treatment for developing countries.

TRIPS (1995)

  • Minimum standards: patents for 20 years in all fields of technology (Art. 27), copyright, trademarks, geographical indications, trade secrets; enforcement.
  • Flexibilities: compulsory licences (Art. 31), exceptions, transition periods.
  • Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health (2001); Art. 31bis (2017) allows export of medicines under compulsory licence.
  • India: the Patents (Amendment) Act, 2005 introduced product patents; s. 3(d) blocks patents for new forms of known substances without enhanced efficacy (Novartis AG v. Union of India, 2013); first compulsory licence to Natco for Bayer's Nexavar (2012).

International investment law

Bilateral investment treaties protect foreign investors (fair and equitable treatment, expropriation with compensation, investor–State arbitration). After awards such as White Industries v. India (2011), India issued a Model BIT (2016) that narrows protections and requires exhausting local remedies, and terminated many older treaties.

Assessment

The system delivered lower tariffs and dispute settlement, but faces a deadlocked Appellate Body, the rise of unilateral tariffs and security exceptions, and calls for reform of IMF and World Bank governance.

Revision points

  • NIEO: Resolutions 1803 (1962), 3201–3202, 3281 (1974).
  • IMF / World Bank roles; weighted voting.
  • GATT Arts. I, III, XI, XX, XXI; WTO 1995; Appellate Body stalled since 2019.
  • TRIPS Art. 31, Doha 2001, Art. 31bis; Novartis (2013), Natco (2012).
Syllabus unit 14 · Asked 16 times · 2014–2026

Answer writing: New International Economic Order and Monetary Law

Decode the demand. Questions test NIEO, Bretton Woods institutions and GATT/WTO principles such as non-discrimination.

Open with

The post-war economic order rested on Bretton Woods institutions and GATT; the NIEO was the developing world's demand to rewrite those rules.

Answer frame

  1. Bretton Woods: IMF and World Bank
  2. GATT principles: MFN, national treatment, exceptions
  3. WTO and dispute settlement
  4. NIEO and the Charter of Economic Rights and Duties
  5. Current crisis: Appellate Body paralysis, unilateral tariffs

Concept in brief

The NIEO (UNGA 1974) and the Charter of Economic Rights and Duties of States sought fairer terms for developing countries and permanent sovereignty over natural resources. The Bretton Woods conference (1944) created the IMF and the World Bank. Trade is governed by GATT 1947, now within the WTO (1995): most-favoured-nation treatment (Art. I) and national treatment (Art. III) are the non-discrimination pillars, subject to exceptions (Art. XX) and special and differential treatment.

Key provisions

  • UNGA Res. 1803 (1962), 3201 and 3281 (1974)
  • IMF and IBRD Articles of Agreement (1944)
  • GATT 1947 Arts. I, III, XI, XX, XXIV
  • WTO Agreement, DSU, TRIPS

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Texaco v. Libya (1977): Stabilisation clauses and resource sovereignty.
  • US – Shrimp (WTO 1998): Art. XX chapeau: no arbitrary discrimination.
  • India – Quantitative Restrictions (WTO 1999): Balance-of-payments restrictions.
  • EC – Hormones (WTO 1998): SPS measures need risk assessment.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Economics: Prebisch–Singer terms-of-trade thesis
  • WTO Appellate Body non-functional since December 2019; the interim MPIA
  • India's positions on special and differential treatment and food security

Critical lens

Non-discrimination is the ideal, but preferential trade agreements and tariffs fragment it.

Close with

A fair economic order needs reformed institutions with a real voice for the Global South.

Previous-year questions (16)

(c) GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)

  1. 2026“The present world economic order is supposed to be guided by the operation of free market forces propelled by free competition, based on free movement of goods and services including technology.” Discuss the role of the principle of non-discrimination and free trade under the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) in this regard. [P-I 6(a), 20 marks]
    How to approach: Discuss the role of non-discrimination: MFN (Art. I), national treatment (Art. III), tariff bindings, exceptions (Arts. XX, XXIV, S&DT). Link to free-market order; critique: preferential deals, unilateral tariffs, Appellate Body crisis.
  2. 2025Explain the principles of 'most favoured nation' and 'national treatment' in the International Trade Law. Is it permissible for a WTO member state to impose different rates of (reciprocal) tariffs on other member states of the WTO? Can a member state affected by the higher tariffs file a complaint with the Dispute Settlement Body of the WTO? Discuss. [7c]
  3. 2020Explain in detail the anti-dumping provisions under GATT and WTO. What are the methods laid down for the determination of "anti-dumping" and "material injury"? [7c]
  4. 2020A WTO member country "X" agrees with a non-member country "Y" to reduce the tariff on product "A" to 7 per cent. Can the WTO members claim the same tariff level on like product "A" from country "X"? How have the dispute settlement bodies defined the "like product" in Article I:1 of GATT? [5b]

(e) IMF (International Monetary Fund) & World Bank

  1. 2026“The Bretton Woods Conference, 1944 adopted the statutes of two institutions to promote New International Economic Order for international financial cooperation and economic development.” Discuss the above statement in terms of the International Forum. [P-I 8(a), 20 marks]
    How to approach: Discuss: Bretton Woods (IMF for monetary stability, IBRD for reconstruction and development), their evolution, and the NIEO critique (voting shares, conditionality). Edge: quota reform and the New Development Bank.
  2. 2024Following World War II destruction, the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund emerged as two historic institutions to promote economic recovery and to build a global monetary system to ensure economic stability around the world. Discuss at length. [7a]
  3. 2018Do you agree with the statement that "the Globalization is a necessary evil"? Critically examine the implications of the reform process undertaken by the IMF and IBRD by way of structural adjustment programmes and policies on developing countries, with special reference to India. [5e]

(a) New International Economic Order (General)

  1. 2024The present world 'Economic Order' is supposed to be granted by the operation of free market forces propelled by free competition and enterprises, based on free movement of goods and services, including technology. Elucidate. [6b]
  2. 2021Discuss the United Nations Declaration on the establishment of a New International Economic Order along with the Charter of Economic Rights and Duties of States. [7b]

(b) WTO (World Trade Organisation)

  1. 2023"WTO provides a platform for agreements amongst its members which form the legal foundation of global trade." Critically evaluate the importance of the WTO in the new international economic order. [8a]
  2. 2022What is the most favoured means of decision-making at the World Trade Organisation? Under what circumstances can decisions be taken by majority votes? Which decisions require super majority votes? Is there a need to reform the decision-making process? Discuss. [8b]
  3. 2018It is generally viewed that "What the U.N. did in the 20th century for maintenance of peace and security, the W.T.O. is going to play the same role on economic and trade relations in the 21st century". Discuss the above statement in view of the changing notion of political sovereignty to economic sovereignty of States. [8c]
  4. 2017The Dispute Settlement Body (DSB) of the World Trade Organisation (WTO) is playing an important role in maintaining the stability of the global economy. Comment. [6b]
  5. 2014What are the objectives, structure and functioning of the World Trade Organisation? Does signing and ratifying the WTO undermine the Parliamentary Autonomy of India? Discuss. [7a]

(d) TRIPS (Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights)

  1. 2018Explain the differences between the Paris and Bern regimes. Do you agree with the statement that "The Trade Related Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPs) is nothing but mere repetition of the Paris and Bern Conventions." Discuss. [7c]
  2. 2014'In several respects the TRIPS Agreement goes beyond the traditional GATT approach and further develops the law of International Trade'. Examine the important achievement of the Agreements on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights. (TRIPS). [6b]
