Study notes

MilestonesCore principlesMajor treatiesRecent advisory opinionsIndia

Milestones

  • Stockholm Conference (1972): Declaration on the Human Environment; Principle 21 (States have sovereign rights over their resources and a responsibility not to cause damage beyond their borders); created UNEP.
  • Brundtland Report (1987): "sustainable development", meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
  • Rio Earth Summit (1992): Rio Declaration (27 principles), Agenda 21, the UNFCCC, the Convention on Biological Diversity, the Forest Principles.
  • Johannesburg (2002) and Rio+20 (2012), which launched the Sustainable Development Goals (adopted 2015).

Core principles

  • No-harm / prevention: Trail Smelter (US v. Canada) (1938, 1941); Principle 21 Stockholm / Principle 2 Rio.
  • Precaution (Rio Principle 15): lack of full scientific certainty is no reason to postpone cost-effective measures.
  • Polluter pays (Principle 16).
  • Environmental impact assessment (Principle 17), a customary obligation for projects with transboundary risk (Pulp Mills (Argentina v. Uruguay), ICJ, 2010).
  • Common but differentiated responsibilities (Principle 7).
  • Sustainable development: Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros (ICJ, 1997).
  • Intergenerational equity; public participation and access to information (Principle 10; Aarhus Convention, 1998).

Major treaties

  • Climate: UNFCCC (1992); Kyoto Protocol (1997) with binding targets only for developed countries; Paris Agreement (2015): keep warming well below 2°C and pursue 1.5°C; nationally determined contributions updated every five years; global stocktake (first in 2023); finance (new collective goal of USD 300 billion a year by 2035 agreed at COP29, Baku, 2024).
  • Ozone: Vienna Convention (1985), Montreal Protocol (1987) and the Kigali Amendment (2016) on HFCs, the most successful environmental treaty.
  • Biodiversity: CBD (1992), Cartagena Protocol (2000, biosafety), Nagoya Protocol (2010, access and benefit sharing), Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (2022) ("30 by 30" target).
  • Others: Ramsar (wetlands, 1971), CITES (1973), Basel (hazardous waste, 1989), Stockholm (POPs, 2001), Minamata (mercury, 2013), BBNJ (high seas biodiversity, 2023). Negotiations on a plastics treaty continue.

Recent advisory opinions

  • ITLOS (2024): greenhouse gas emissions are marine pollution under UNCLOS; States must take all necessary measures with stringent due diligence.
  • ICJ, Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change (July 2025, unanimous): States have obligations under the climate treaties and customary law (due diligence, prevention, cooperation) and under human rights law; breaching them is an internationally wrongful act that can entail State responsibility. The 1.5°C goal is the primary temperature goal under the Paris framework.
  • Inter-American Court of Human Rights, Advisory Opinion OC-32/25 (2025) on the climate emergency and human rights.

India

  • Constitution: Arts. 48A and 51A(g); Art. 21 includes the right to a healthy environment.
  • Cases: Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum (1996: precaution and polluter pays are part of Indian law); M.C. Mehta cases (absolute liability, Ganga, vehicular pollution); T.N. Godavarman (forests); M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (2024): a right against the adverse effects of climate change under Arts. 14 and 21.
  • Laws: Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; National Green Tribunal Act, 2010; Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (amended 2023); Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 (carbon credit trading).
  • India's NDC (updated 2022): reduce emissions intensity of GDP by 45% from 2005 levels by 2030; about 50% of installed power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030; net zero by 2070.

Revision points

  • Stockholm 1972 → Brundtland 1987 → Rio 1992 → Paris 2015.
  • Principles: no-harm (Trail Smelter), precaution, polluter pays, EIA (Pulp Mills), CBDR.
  • Montreal + Kigali; Kunming-Montreal GBF 2022.
  • ITLOS AO 2024; ICJ climate AO 2025; Ranjitsinh (2024).
Syllabus unit 15 · Asked 9 times · 2015–2026

Answer writing: Protection and Improvement of the Human Environment: International Efforts

Decode the demand. Questions trace milestones (Stockholm → Rio → Kyoto → Paris) and principles. A timeline earns marks.

Open with

International environmental law grew from Stockholm's warning (1972) into a dense web of principles and treaties, now reinforced by the ICJ's 2025 climate opinion.

Answer frame

  1. Milestones before Kyoto
  2. Kyoto Protocol: targets and mechanisms
  3. After Kyoto: Copenhagen, Paris and the COPs
  4. Principles: precaution, CBDR-RC, polluter pays
  5. Judicial turn: ITLOS 2024, ICJ 2025

Concept in brief

Stockholm (1972) launched international environmental law; Rio (1992) gave sustainable development, precaution (Principle 15) and polluter pays (Principle 16). The UNFCCC (1992), Kyoto Protocol (1997, binding targets for Annex I countries with market mechanisms) and Paris Agreement (2015, NDCs) form the climate regime. The ICJ's advisory opinion of July 2025 confirmed binding State obligations on climate change.

Key provisions

  • Stockholm Declaration 1972
  • Rio Declaration 1992
  • UNFCCC 1992, Kyoto 1997, Paris 2015
  • Montreal Protocol 1987 (Kigali 2016)
  • CBD 1992

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Trail Smelter (US v. Canada) (1941): No State may allow its territory to cause serious transboundary harm.
  • Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros (1997): Sustainable development recognised.
  • Pulp Mills (Argentina v. Uruguay) (2010): EIA is a customary obligation.
  • Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change (ICJ AO, 23 July 2025): Unanimous: States owe binding duties to prevent significant climate harm.
  • Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996): Precaution and polluter pays are part of Indian law.
  • M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (2024): Right against the adverse effects of climate change under Arts. 14 and 21.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • India's net-zero 2070 target and Mission LiFE
  • Climate finance gaps
  • Vellore and Ranjitsinh as the domestic counterpart

Draw this

Stockholm 1972 → Brundtland 1987 → Rio 1992 → Kyoto 1997 → Copenhagen 2009 → Paris 2015 → ICJ AO 2025

Critical lens

Voluntary pledges under Paris lack teeth; equity between North and South remains unresolved.

Close with

Climate law is moving from pledges to obligations; implementation and finance will decide its success.

Previous-year questions (9)
  1. 2026Discuss briefly ‘pre and post’ developments of Kyoto Environment Summit on Global Warming, 1997 for the protection and improvement of the human environment. [P-I 8(b), 15 marks]
    How to approach: Timeline answer. Before Kyoto: Stockholm, Brundtland, Rio and the UNFCCC, Berlin Mandate. Kyoto: Annex I targets, flexibility mechanisms. After Kyoto: Marrakesh, Doha amendment, Paris NDCs, recent COPs, ICJ AO 2025. India's stance on CBDR.
  2. 2023"Member States of the UN need to take appropriate action for protecting and improving the human environment." In light of the above statement, highlight the major steps of the UN for protecting the human environment. [8b]
  3. 2021Discuss the role of the United Nations in the protection and improvement of the human environment. [8c]
  4. 2020Evaluate the main sources of International Environmental Law. Explain and discuss in particular the emergence of "Soft Law" and principles of International Environmental Law, and how this has influenced the development of this area of International Law. [8b]
  5. 2018Do you agree with the statement that "Beginning with the Stockholm Declaration of 1972, there has been an increased reliance upon non-binding international instruments dealing with the environment"? Why has this trend developed, and have these instruments been more useful than treaties? Explain. [8b]
  6. 2018(i): Write critical notes on International efforts towards the protection and improvement of the human environment. [8a]
  7. 2017What do you mean by 'human environment'? Discuss the role of the United Nations Organisation (UNO) in protecting and improving the human environment. [7a]
  8. 2016The Final words of the Paris Agreement under the UNFCCC, 2015, were adopted unanimously by 195 countries. According to this Agreement, Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) are to be reported every 5 years and are to be registered with UNFCCC Secretariat which will be 'progressive' depending upon the targets set by each country itself and therefore contributions have been made 'non-binding' as a matter of International Law and there will be a 'name and shame system' or 'name and encourage plan'. After explaining essential features, comment on the effectiveness of such an Agreement. [8c]
  9. 2015Critically evaluate the laws/conventions/practices available for the protection and preservation of the marine environment under International law. Also discuss the rules provided under International law for 'transit passage' and its abuses. [7a]
