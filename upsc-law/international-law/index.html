UPSC CSE Law Optional · Paper I

International Law: UPSC Law Optional notes and PYQs

All 15 syllabus units of International Law, in syllabus order. Each unit page has full study notes, then how to decode the question, frame the answer and which provisions and cases to cite, and every previous-year question from 2013 to 2026 (188 questions in all).

UPSC Law Optional desk · Paper I

Unit 1 · Asked 12 times · 2013–2026

Nature and definition of international law

Two set questions recur: is international law 'law', and define it with its sources. Present Austin's objection, then the rebuttals.

Cases: S.S. Lotus (France v. Turkey) (PCIJ 1927), North Sea Continental Shelf (1969), Asylum (Colombia v. Peru) (1950)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 2 · Asked 12 times · 2013–2026

International law and municipal law

Questions ask for theories (monism, dualism, coordination) and Indian practice. Keep the theory brief and give the Indian position in depth.

Cases: Trendtex Trading v. Central Bank of Nigeria (1977), Maganbhai Ishwarbhai Patel v. Union of India (1969), Jolly George Varghese v. Bank of Cochin (1980)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 3 · Asked 15 times · 2013–2026

State recognition and State succession

Expect theories of recognition and the difference between recognition and succession. Use modern examples.

Cases: Tinoco Arbitration (UK v. Costa Rica) (1923), Luther v. Sagor (1921), Burkina Faso/Mali Frontier Dispute (1986)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 4 · Asked 20 times · 2013–2026

Law of the sea (UNCLOS)

Very frequently asked. Questions test zones and passage regimes, often through a case or a current choke-point. Draw a zone diagram.

Cases: Corfu Channel (UK v. Albania) (1949), Anglo-Norwegian Fisheries (1951), North Sea Continental Shelf (1969)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 5 · Asked 16 times · 2013–2026

Individuals: nationality, statelessness, human rights

Questions test nationality (modes, nationality vs domicile, statelessness) and enforcement of human rights. Give mechanisms, not just rights.

Cases: Nottebohm (Liechtenstein v. Guatemala) (1955), Barcelona Traction (1970), LaGrand (Germany v. USA) (2001)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 6 · Asked 15 times · 2013–2026

Territorial jurisdiction, extradition and asylum

Questions ask about jurisdiction principles, extradition and asylum, often 'is asylum a right?'. Distinguish the right to seek from the right to receive.

Cases: S.S. Lotus (PCIJ 1927), Asylum (Colombia v. Peru) (1950), Savarkar (France v. Great Britain) (PCA 1911)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 7 · Asked 13 times · 2013–2025

Law of treaties (VCLT 1969)

Expect formation and reservations, pacta sunt servanda and rebus sic stantibus. Use VCLT articles as headings.

Cases: Reservations to the Genocide Convention (1951), Fisheries Jurisdiction (UK v. Iceland) (1973), Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros (1997)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 8 · Asked 12 times · 2015–2026

United Nations: organs, Security Council, reform

Questions test Security Council powers, the veto (including the double veto), and UN reform.

Cases: Reparation for Injuries (1949), Certain Expenses of the UN (1962), Namibia Advisory Opinion (1971)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 9 · Asked 14 times · 2013–2025

Peaceful settlement of international disputes

Expect modes of settlement and the ICJ's limits. Organise by diplomatic vs legal methods.

Cases: Mavrommatis Palestine Concessions (PCIJ 1924), Rann of Kutch Arbitration (India/Pakistan) (1968), Indus Waters Kishenganga Arbitration (PCA 2013)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 10 · Asked 10 times · 2013–2026

Lawful recourse to force and self-defence (Art. 2(4), 51)

Questions ask about aggression, self-defence or intervention, often combined. Use Art. 2(4) and Art. 51 as anchors and test any example against necessity and proportionality.

Cases: Caroline incident (1837), Corfu Channel (1949), Nicaragua v. USA (1986)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 11 · Asked 9 times · 2014–2023

International humanitarian law

Questions ask for core principles and recent developments. Name the principles and apply them to a current conflict neutrally.

Cases: Prosecutor v. Tadić (ICTY 1995), Legality of Nuclear Weapons (1996), Rev. Mons. Sebastiao Francisco Xavier dos Remedios Monteiro v. State of Goa (1970)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 12 · Asked 5 times · 2017–2025

Nuclear weapons, NPT and CTBT

Expect the ICJ 1996 opinion and the NPT and CTBT regime, plus India's position.

Cases: Legality of the Threat or Use of Nuclear Weapons (1996), Nuclear Tests (Australia v. France) (1974), Marshall Islands v. India (2016)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 13 · Asked 10 times · 2015–2025

International terrorism, hijacking and the ICC

Questions test the definitional gap in terrorism, hijacking conventions and the ICC. Show both the legal framework and India's role.

Cases: Lockerbie (Libya v. UK/USA) (1992), Prosecutor v. Thomas Lubanga (ICC 2012)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 14 · Asked 16 times · 2014–2026

NIEO, WTO and international monetary law

Questions test NIEO, Bretton Woods institutions and GATT/WTO principles such as non-discrimination.

Cases: Texaco v. Libya (1977), US – Shrimp (WTO 1998), India – Quantitative Restrictions (WTO 1999)

Study notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 15 · Asked 9 times · 2015–2026

International environmental law

Questions trace milestones (Stockholm → Rio → Kyoto → Paris) and principles. A timeline earns marks.

Cases: Trail Smelter (US v. Canada) (1941), Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros (1997), Pulp Mills (Argentina v. Uruguay) (2010)
