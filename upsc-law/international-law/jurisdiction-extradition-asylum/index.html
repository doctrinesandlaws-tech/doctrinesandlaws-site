Territorial jurisdiction, extradition and asylum

International Law for the UPSC Civil Services (Main) Law optional: how to decode the question, an answer frame, key provisions, leading cases and 15 previous-year questions (2013–2026).

All 15 units of International Law · Jump to the questions

Syllabus unit 6 · Asked 15 times · 2013–2026

Syllabus: Territorial jurisdiction of States, Extradition, and Asylum

Decode the demand. Questions ask about jurisdiction principles, extradition and asylum, often 'is asylum a right?'. Distinguish the right to seek from the right to receive.

Open with

Jurisdiction marks the reach of a State's law; extradition and asylum mark the limits of that reach across borders.

Answer frame

  1. Bases of jurisdiction
  2. Extradition principles and Indian law
  3. Asylum: territorial vs diplomatic
  4. Is asylum a right? (UDHR Art. 14, non-refoulement)
  5. Indian practice

Concept in brief

A State has jurisdiction on territorial, nationality, protective, passive-personality and universality grounds. Extradition rests on treaties and reciprocity, with double criminality, speciality and the political-offence exception. Asylum is territorial (granted within the State) or diplomatic (in embassies, accepted only regionally). The UDHR gives a right to seek asylum, not a right to be granted it; non-refoulement protects refugees.

Key provisions

  • Extradition Act, 1962
  • UDHR Art. 14
  • Refugee Convention 1951, Art. 33 (India not a party)

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • S.S. Lotus (PCIJ 1927): Objective territorial jurisdiction.
  • Asylum (Colombia v. Peru) (1950): Diplomatic asylum is not general custom.
  • Savarkar (France v. Great Britain) (PCA 1911): No duty to return a fugitive irregularly recaptured.
  • Abu Salem v. State of Maharashtra (2010): Rule of speciality binds Indian prosecutors.
  • NHRC v. State of Arunachal Pradesh (1996): Chakma refugees protected under Art. 21.
  • Mohammad Salimullah v. Union of India (2021): Non-refoulement claims of Rohingya; no interim stay on deportation.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • India hosts Tibetan, Sri Lankan Tamil and other refugees without a refugee statute
  • Comparative: EU Common European Asylum System
  • Recent extradition debates over fugitive economic offenders

Critical lens

Without a national refugee law, protection in India depends on executive discretion.

Close with

Asylum remains a State's right to grant, softened by non-refoulement's duty not to return.

Previous-year questions (15)

(c) Asylum

  1. 2026What is ‘asylum’? Is ‘Right to Asylum’ a fundamental right of a person in other country to save from prosecution? Explain. [P-I 5(c), 10 marks]
    How to approach: Two parts. Define territorial and diplomatic asylum; UDHR Art. 14 gives a right to seek, not to be granted; no fundamental right to asylum in India (non-citizens have Art. 21, not entry rights; Salimullah). Mention non-refoulement and the 1951 Convention.
  2. 2025Discuss the different types of 'Asylum' and make a distinction between Territorial and Extraterritorial Asylum. [8c]
  3. 2022What are Territorial Asylum and Extraterritorial Asylum? Explain. [5c]
  4. 2017Discuss the scope of the right of asylum under International Law and explain 'territorial' and 'extra-territorial' asylum. [8b]
  5. 2015What is the meaning of the term 'Territorial Asylum', under International law? What are its major components? And how is it different from the term "Territorial Sovereignty'? Discuss. [7c]

(d) Extradition and Asylum

  1. 2024Discuss the law on extradition. The procedure for granting asylum and approving extradition requests differs significantly. Explain. [7b]
  2. 2020"Asylum ends where extradition begins." Comment. Distinguish between territorial asylum and extra-territorial asylum. [6b]
  3. 2014"Asylum stops as it were when extradition begins". Comment. Also, explain the various principles of extradition with reference to leading cases. [8a]

(b) Extradition

  1. 2021Explain the principle of 'Double Criminality' and the 'Rule of Speciality' under the international law of extradition. [5c]
  2. 2018"Where Extradition begins Asylum ends." Critically examine the above statement with special reference to extraditable persons and extradition crimes. [5b]
  3. 2016Can India invoke the India-UK Mutual Legal Assistance Agreement for extraditing an Indian national who has run away to the UK in spite of an Indian Court order in respect of fraud and money laundering against him? Explain. [6b]
  4. 2015Evaluate the importance and relevance of the 'Extradition Treaty', under International law. Is a state liable to extradite an offender who has been accused of a political murder in a neighbouring state? Give reasons. [5b]
  5. 2013A group of three men (L, M and N), citizens of country A...flee away to country B... The Government of A, requests B to extradite L, M and N...B declines. A sends spies to B who abduct L, M and N...‘B’ approaches ICJ against use of force by ‘A’. Prepare (i) A brief of arguments for A, (ii) A brief of arguments for B, and (iii) the Opinion of the Court. [7b]

(a) Territorial jurisdiction of States

  1. 2018What do you understand by the territorial jurisdiction of States? Do you agree with the view that the Right to Seek Asylum is firmly established under International Human Rights Law? If so, substantiate your answer with reasons. [7a]
  2. 2014International Law sets little or no limitation on the jurisdiction which a particular State may arrogate to itself. Explain the nature and scope of 'State Jurisdiction'. Critically examine the principles of 'State Jurisdiction'. [5b]
