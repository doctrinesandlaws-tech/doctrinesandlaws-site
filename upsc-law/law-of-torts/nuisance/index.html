Meaning

Nuisance is an unlawful interference with a person's use or enjoyment of land, or of some right over or in connection with it.

Public and private nuisance

Public nuisancePrivate nuisance
AffectsThe public or a section of itA particular person's use of land
NatureCrime (BNS s. 270; IPC s. 268) and tortTort only
Who can sueAdvocate-General or two persons with court leave (CPC s. 91); an individual only with special damageThe person whose interest in land is affected
ExamplesObstructing a highway, polluting a riverNoise, smoke, smells, encroaching roots

Private nuisance: essentials

  1. Unreasonable interference with use or enjoyment of land or rights (easements).
  2. Damage: either material injury to property, or substantial interference with comfort.
  3. St Helen's Smelting Co. v. Tipping (1865): material injury to property is actionable regardless of the locality; for sensible personal discomfort, the character of the neighbourhood matters.
  4. Reasonableness factors: locality, duration, intensity, sensitivity of the plaintiff, and malice. - Robinson v. Kilvert (1889): no liability for damage to unusually sensitive paper. - Christie v. Davey (1893) and Hollywood Silver Fox Farm v. Emmett (1936): malicious noise makes an otherwise lawful act a nuisance. - Sturges v. Bridgman (1879): "coming to the nuisance" is no defence.

Who is liable

The creator of the nuisance, the occupier (even for nuisances created by others or by natural causes if he adopts or continues them: Sedleigh-Denfield v. O'Callaghan, 1940), and in some cases the landlord.

Defences

  • Prescription: 20 years' continuous enjoyment (Limitation Act, 1963, and Indian Easements Act, 1882, s. 15) for private nuisance; not for public nuisance.
  • Statutory authority.
  • Not defences: public benefit, coming to the nuisance, that many contributed to it, that the defendant used reasonable care.

Remedies

  • Damages and injunction (the usual remedy).
  • Abatement (self-help removal, with notice where needed).
  • Public nuisance: magistrate's order under BNSS s. 152 (CrPC s. 133). Municipal Council, Ratlam v. Vardhichand (1980): the municipality was ordered to fix drains and sanitation; lack of funds is no excuse.

Indian applications

  • Ram Raj Singh v. Babulal (1982): dust from a brick-grinding machine near a doctor's clinic.
  • Dhannalal v. Thakur Chittarsingh (1959): noise from a flour mill.
  • In re Noise Pollution (2005): restrictions on loudspeakers and firecrackers at night; the right to sleep is part of Art. 21.
  • Environmental nuisance often overlaps with pollution laws and NGT jurisdiction.

Revision points

  • Public vs private nuisance table; special damage for individuals in public nuisance.
  • St Helen's (material injury vs discomfort); Robinson v. Kilvert; Christie v. Davey; Sturges v. Bridgman.
  • Defences: prescription (20 years), statutory authority.
  • Ratlam (1980) under CrPC s. 133 (BNSS s. 152).
Syllabus unit 9 · Asked 8 times · 2013–2026

Answer writing: Nuisance

Decode the demand. Questions test public vs private nuisance, special damage and environmental links.

Open with

Nuisance protects the use and enjoyment of land and public rights; one act can do both when an individual suffers special damage.

Answer frame

  1. Private nuisance: elements, reasonableness
  2. Public nuisance: crime and tort
  3. Special damage bridge (Castle v St Augustine's)
  4. Defences and remedies
  5. Environmental nuisance in India

Concept in brief

Nuisance is unlawful interference with a person's use or enjoyment of land (private) or an act affecting the public at large (public). The same act can be both: a public nuisance becomes privately actionable where the plaintiff suffers special damage beyond the public. Public nuisance is also a crime and a ground for magisterial orders.

Key provisions

  • CPC s. 91
  • IPC s. 268 → BNS s. 270
  • CrPC s. 133 → BNSS s. 152

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Attorney-General v. PYA Quarries (1957): Public nuisance affects a class of people.
  • Castle v. St. Augustine's Links (1922): Golf balls on a highway: public nuisance with special damage.
  • Radhey Shyam v. Gur Prasad Sharma (1978): Noise as private nuisance.
  • Dr. Ram Raj Singh v. Babulal (1982): Brick-grinding dust held nuisance.
  • Municipal Council, Ratlam v. Vardhichand (1980): Public nuisance duties of municipalities.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Economics: the Coase theorem on externalities
  • Noise rules, 2000 and the 'In re Noise Pollution' case (2005)
  • Ratlam as an early environmental PIL

Critical lens

Private actions are rare; public-law remedies now dominate.

Close with

Nuisance law remains the common-law root of environmental protection.
