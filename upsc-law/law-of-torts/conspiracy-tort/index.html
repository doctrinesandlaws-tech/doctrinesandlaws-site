Conspiracy (tort)

Meaning

The tort of conspiracy is a combination of two or more persons to injure another, which causes damage. It has two forms.

Lawful means conspiracy

  • An agreement to use lawful means with the predominant purpose of injuring the plaintiff (not to further the conspirators' own legitimate interests).
  • Mogul Steamship Co. v. McGregor, Gow & Co. (1892): shipowners combined to offer low freight rates and drive out a competitor; no conspiracy because they acted to protect their trade.
  • Quinn v. Leathem (1901): a trade union official threatened a butcher's customers to make him dismiss non-union workers; liable because the object was to injure.
  • Crofter Hand Woven Harris Tweed Co. v. Veitch (1942): union officials who imposed an embargo on imported yarn were not liable; their predominant purpose was to protect members' interests.

Unlawful means conspiracy

  • An agreement to use unlawful means (crimes, torts, breach of contract) with an intention to injure the plaintiff, which need not be the predominant purpose.
  • Lonrho plc v. Fayed (1992): intention to injure is needed, but not as the predominant purpose.
  • Revenue and Customs Commissioners v. Total Network SL (2008): a criminal act (VAT fraud) can be unlawful means even if not separately actionable.

Essentials

  1. An agreement (combination) of two or more persons.
  2. An intention to injure the plaintiff (predominant, for lawful means).
  3. Acts done in pursuance of the agreement.
  4. Actual damage (it is not actionable per se).

Defences

Justification: protection of a legitimate interest (trade, employment, collective bargaining).

Trade unions

  • In India, s. 18 of the Trade Unions Act, 1926 gives a registered trade union and its members immunity from civil suits for acts in furtherance of a trade dispute on the ground only that the act induces breach of a contract of employment or interferes with trade. s. 17 gives immunity from criminal conspiracy for agreements to further the union's lawful objects.
  • Rohtas Industries v. Rohtas Industries Staff Union (1976): the Supreme Court discussed tort liability for an illegal strike; such claims are hard to sustain.
  • The UK Trade Disputes Act, 1906 was the model; the UK later narrowed immunities.

Relation to other torts

Conspiracy overlaps with inducing breach of contract (Lumley v. Gye, 1853) and causing loss by unlawful means (OBG v. Allan, 2007). Criminal conspiracy (BNS s. 61) does not require damage; the tort does.

Revision points

  • Lawful means (predominant purpose to injure) vs unlawful means (intention enough).
  • Mogul (1892), Quinn v. Leathem (1901), Crofter (1942), Lonrho v. Fayed (1992), Total Network (2008).
  • Damage essential (unlike criminal conspiracy).
  • Trade Unions Act ss. 17–18 immunities.

Answer writing: Conspiracy

Decode the demand. Rarely asked. Know the two forms and the predominant-purpose test.

Open with

Conspiracy in tort punishes combinations that cause damage either by unlawful means or with the predominant purpose of injury.

Answer frame

  1. Two forms
  2. Elements and damage
  3. Cases
  4. Overlap with economic torts

Concept in brief

The tort of conspiracy has two forms: a lawful-means conspiracy whose predominant purpose is to injure the claimant, and an unlawful-means conspiracy where the means are themselves unlawful. Damage must result.

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Crofter Hand Woven Harris Tweed v. Veitch (1942): Predominant purpose test.
  • Quinn v. Leathem (1901): Combination to injure actionable.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Trade union disputes as the historic context

Critical lens

The tort is narrow and uncertain at its edges.

Close with

It survives as a remedy for coordinated economic harm.
