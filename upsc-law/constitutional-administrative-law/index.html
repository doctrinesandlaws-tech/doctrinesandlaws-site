Constitutional and Administrative Law: UPSC Law Optional notes and PYQs

All 16 syllabus units of Constitutional and Administrative Law, in syllabus order. Each unit page shows how to decode the question, frame the answer, which provisions and cases to cite, and every previous-year question from 2013 to 2026 (194 questions in all).

UPSC Law Optional desk · Paper I

Unit 1 · Asked 12 times · 2014–2026

Constitutionalism and salient features of the Constitution

Examiners rarely ask for a list of features. They ask you to separate the document (Constitution) from the idea (constitutionalism), or to classify Indian federalism. Show that India has both a…

Cases: Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), State of West Bengal v. Union of India (1962), S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 2 · Asked 33 times · 2013–2026

Fundamental Rights (Art. 12–35), PIL and legal aid

This is the most-asked unit. Questions test a doctrine's evolution (Art. 12 tests, eclipse and severability, the golden triangle, reservation), or ask you to critique judicial creativity. Always trace…

Cases: Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981), Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Indian Institute of Chemical Biology (2002), Zee Telefilms v. Union of India (2005)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 3 · Asked 13 times · 2013–2026

Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Fundamental Duties

Questions ask how FRs, DPSPs and Fundamental Duties fit together, or test definitions such as 'State' under Art. 36. Show the shift from conflict to harmony with dated cases.

Cases: State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951), Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980), Unni Krishnan J.P. v. State of A.P. (1993)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 4 · Asked 13 times · 2014–2026

President and Council of Ministers (Art. 52–78)

Expect questions on how far the President is bound by advice, on the election and disputes procedure, or on pardon and ordinances. Answer with constitutional articles first, then cases.

Cases: Ram Jawaya Kapur v. State of Punjab (1955), Samsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974), U.N.R. Rao v. Indira Gandhi (1971)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 5 · Asked 6 times · 2015–2025

Governor: position and powers (Art. 153–167)

The current flashpoint is gubernatorial delay over Bills. Expect to analyse discretion vs advice and to discuss reforms.

Cases: B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010), Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006), Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 6 · Asked 11 times · 2014–2026

Supreme Court and High Courts: appointments, collegium, jurisdiction

Questions test appointment (collegium vs NJAC) or the breadth of the Supreme Court's jurisdiction. Use article numbers as anchors and evaluate judicial independence against accountability.

Cases: S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (1981), S.C. Advocates-on-Record Assn. v. Union of India (1993), In re Special Reference 1 of 1998

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 7 · Asked 22 times · 2013–2025

Centre–State relations and local bodies

Questions test distribution of legislative power and interpretation doctrines, local government, or eminent domain. Use doctrines as headings and a recent federal dispute as the hook.

Cases: Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee v. Bank of Commerce, Khulna (1947, PC), K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. State of Orissa (1953), M. Karunanidhi v. Union of India (1979)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 8 · Asked 7 times · 2017–2026

Legislative powers, privileges and immunities (Art. 105, 194)

Questions cover privileges vs fundamental rights, bribery and immunity, and the legislative process including Money Bills and Parliament's power over UTs. State the article, then the tension with Part III.

Cases: M.S.M. Sharma v. Sri Krishna Sinha (1959), In re Keshav Singh (Special Reference 1 of 1964), Tej Kiran Jain v. N. Sanjiva Reddy (1970)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 9 · Asked 16 times · 2014–2026

Services under the Union and States (Art. 308–323)

Expect the doctrine of pleasure and Art. 311 safeguards, tribunals, and the independence of UPSC and the Election Commission. Show that pleasure is constitutionally qualified.

Cases: Parshotam Lal Dhingra v. Union of India (1958), Moti Ram Deka v. N.E. Frontier Railway (1964), Union of India v. Tulsiram Patel (1985)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 10 · Asked 8 times · 2013–2026

Emergency provisions (Art. 352, 356, 360)

Questions test the 44th Amendment safeguards and judicial review of Art. 356. Contrast the 1975 Emergency with present safeguards.

Cases: ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla (1976), State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977), Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 11 · Asked 9 times · 2013–2026

Amendment of the Constitution and basic structure (Art. 368)

Always asked as an evolution: Shankari Prasad → Golaknath → Kesavananda → Minerva → Coelho/NJAC. Evaluate legitimacy of the basic-structure doctrine.

Cases: Shankari Prasad v. Union of India (1951), Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1965), I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 12 · Asked 9 times · 2016–2026

Principles of natural justice

Questions test the flexible content of natural justice, its exclusion, and post-decisional hearing. Use maxims, then the prejudice test.

Cases: Ridge v. Baldwin (1964, HL), A.K. Kraipak v. Union of India (1969), Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 13 · Asked 9 times · 2015–2026

Delegated legislation and its constitutionality

Questions ask for the limits of permissible delegation and controls on it. Lead with the essential-function test and support it with cases.

Cases: In re Delhi Laws Act (1951), Hamdard Dawakhana v. Union of India (1960), Devi Das Gopal Krishnan v. State of Punjab (1967)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 14 · Asked 7 times · 2013–2026

Separation of powers

Expect separation of powers in India and constitutional governance, including the Sixth Schedule. Show functional separation with checks and balances.

Cases: Ram Jawaya Kapur v. State of Punjab (1955), Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975), State of T.N. v. State of Kerala (2014)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 15 · Asked 11 times · 2016–2026

Judicial review of administrative action

Questions test grounds of review, proportionality vs Wednesbury, and the place of judicial review in the basic structure.

Cases: Associated Provincial Picture Houses v. Wednesbury Corp. (1948), Council of Civil Service Unions v. Minister for the Civil Service (1985), Barium Chemicals v. Company Law Board (1967)

Notes, answer frame and questions →Unit 16 · Asked 8 times · 2015–2026

Ombudsman: Lokpal and Lokayukta

Questions examine the ombudsman's role and the Lokpal's effectiveness. Pair the Scandinavian theory with Indian data and gaps.

Cases: Common Cause v. Union of India (2017), Justice Chandrashekaraiah (Retd.) v. Janekere C. Krishna (2013)
