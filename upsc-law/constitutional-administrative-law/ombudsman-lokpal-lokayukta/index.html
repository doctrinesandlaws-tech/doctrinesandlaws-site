The ombudsman idea

An ombudsman is an independent officer who receives complaints against public administration and investigates maladministration and corruption. The office began in Sweden (1809) and spread to Finland, Denmark, New Zealand and the UK (Parliamentary Commissioner for Administration, 1967).

Indian history

  • The First Administrative Reforms Commission (1966) recommended a Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayuktas in the States.
  • Lokpal bills lapsed eight times between 1968 and 2011.
  • Maharashtra created the first Lokayukta (1971).
  • The 2011 anti-corruption movement led to the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (in force 16 January 2014). The first Lokpal, Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose, was appointed in March 2019.

Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013: key features

  • Composition: a Chairperson and up to eight members; at least half judicial members; at least half from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women.
  • Selection committee: Prime Minister, Speaker of the Lok Sabha, Leader of the Opposition (or leader of the largest opposition party), Chief Justice of India or a nominated judge, and an eminent jurist nominated by the others. Common Cause v. Union of India (2017): appointments need not wait for a recognised Leader of the Opposition.
  • Jurisdiction: the Prime Minister (with exclusions for international relations, external and internal security, public order, atomic energy and space; inquiry needs a full bench and two-thirds approval, held in camera), Union ministers, MPs (except for speech and votes protected by Art. 105), Group A–D officials, and bodies financed or controlled by government or receiving foreign contributions above a threshold.
  • Machinery: an Inquiry Wing and a Prosecution Wing; power to direct CBI and supervise investigations referred to it; special courts to finish trials within a year.
  • Assets: public servants must declare assets (s. 44, amended in 2016).
  • Lokayuktas: s. 63 required States to set up Lokayuktas within 365 days; States have discretion over their design.
  • In 2025 the Supreme Court took up and stayed a Lokpal order that had treated sitting High Court judges as within its jurisdiction, pending a decision on the question.

Related integrity institutions

  • Central Vigilance Commission: given statutory status by the CVC Act, 2003, after Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998) directed independence for CBI and CVC.
  • Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (amended 2018): s. 17A requires prior approval to investigate decisions taken in official functions.
  • Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 (not operationalised fully).
  • Right to Information Act, 2005 supports transparency.

Assessment

  • Strengths: statutory independence, inclusion of the Prime Minister, time-bound processes, a single forum for complaints.
  • Weaknesses: few complaints actually pursued; vacancies and delays in appointments; dependence on CBI for investigation; exclusion of the judiciary; weak or ineffective Lokayuktas in many States; protection for honest officers versus effective deterrence.
  • Way forward: functional and financial autonomy, own investigation cadre, uniform minimum standards for State Lokayuktas, and operationalising whistle-blower protection.

Revision points

  • Sweden 1809 → ARC 1966 → Act 2013 → first Lokpal 2019.
  • Selection committee and the Common Cause (2017) clarification.
  • PM within Lokpal with exclusions; s. 63 for State Lokayuktas.
  • Vineet Narain (1998) for CBI/CVC independence.
Syllabus unit 16 · Asked 8 times · 2015–2026

Answer writing: Ombudsman: Lokayukta, Lokpal, etc

Decode the demand. Questions examine the ombudsman's role and the Lokpal's effectiveness. Pair the Scandinavian theory with Indian data and gaps.

Open with

The ombudsman, born in Sweden in 1809, is the legislature's eye on administration, an independent watchdog for citizens' grievances.

Answer frame

  1. Concept and features
  2. Indian evolution: 1st ARC (1966), Lokpal Bills, Lokpal Act 2013
  3. Structure, jurisdiction and safeguards
  4. Performance and gaps
  5. Way forward

Concept in brief

An ombudsman is an independent officer who investigates complaints of maladministration on behalf of the legislature, originating in Sweden (1809). India's Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 created the Lokpal for central public servants, including the Prime Minister with safeguards, and required States to set up Lokayuktas. The first Lokpal was appointed in 2019.

Key provisions

  • Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (ss. 3, 4, 14, 20, 63)
  • Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Common Cause v. Union of India (2017): Lokpal Act workable without a recognised Leader of Opposition; appointments to proceed.
  • Justice Chandrashekaraiah (Retd.) v. Janekere C. Krishna (2013): Consultation with the Chief Justice in appointing the Karnataka Upa-Lokayukta.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • 1st ARC (1966) recommendation of a Lokpal and Lokayuktas
  • 2nd ARC 4th Report on ethics in governance
  • Comparative: UK Parliamentary Ombudsman (filter through MPs)
  • Data: Lokpal complaint disposal (annual reports)

Critical lens

Delayed appointments, a narrow suo motu role and dependence on investigating agencies blunt the institution.

Close with

An ombudsman matters only when independent, accessible and quick; otherwise it becomes another layer of delay.

    How to approach: Elucidate with examples. Ombudsman as Parliament's agent supervising administration (Swedish model); India: Lokpal Act 2013, Lokayuktas (Maharashtra 1972, Karnataka). Gaps and reforms. Edge: 1st and 2nd ARC.

Critically examine this statement by providing proper justification of the office of the 'Lokpal' in India. [1b]
