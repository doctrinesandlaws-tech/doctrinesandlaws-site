Amendment of the Constitution and basic structure (Art. 368)

Syllabus unit 11 · Asked 9 times · 2013–2026

Syllabus: Amendment of the Constitution

Decode the demand. Always asked as an evolution: Shankari Prasad → Golaknath → Kesavananda → Minerva → Coelho/NJAC. Evaluate legitimacy of the basic-structure doctrine.

Open with

The basic-structure doctrine is India's distinctive answer to how a constitution can be amendable yet protect its identity.

Answer frame

  1. Art. 368 procedure
  2. Phase I: unlimited power (Shankari Prasad, Sajjan Singh)
  3. Phase II: Golaknath's bar on abridging Fundamental Rights
  4. Phase III: Kesavananda's implied limits
  5. Consolidation and critique

Concept in brief

Art. 368 gives Parliament power to amend by special majority (and State ratification for federal provisions). After Shankari Prasad and Sajjan Singh allowed amendment of Fundamental Rights, Golaknath (1967) said Parliament could not abridge them. Kesavananda (1973) replaced that rigid bar with the basic-structure limit, which is both a refinement of Golaknath and a creative judicial check that later cases have applied.

Key provisions

  • Art. 368
  • Art. 13(4) (24th Amendment)
  • Art. 31B, Ninth Schedule

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Shankari Prasad v. Union of India (1951): Amendment is not 'law' under Art. 13.
  • Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1965): Followed Shankari Prasad; dissent doubted it.
  • I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967): Fundamental Rights cannot be abridged by amendment (prospective overruling).
  • Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973): Basic structure doctrine.
  • Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975): Free and fair elections, judicial review are basic features; cl. 4 of 39th Amendment struck down.
  • Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980): Art. 368(4), (5) struck down.
  • Waman Rao v. Union of India (1981): Basic structure applies prospectively from 24.4.1973.
  • S.C. Advocates-on-Record Assn. v. Union of India (2015): Judicial independence is basic structure; NJAC struck down.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Comparative: German Basic Law Art. 79(3) 'eternity clause'; Bangladesh's Anwar Hossain (1989) adopting the doctrine
  • Theory: constituent vs constituted power (Sieyès); Dietrich Conrad's influence
  • Political context: 24th, 25th, 39th and 42nd Amendments

Draw this

Shankari Prasad (1951) → Golaknath (1967) → 24th Amdt (1971) → Kesavananda (1973) → Minerva (1980) → Coelho (2007)

Critical lens

Critics call it counter-majoritarian and indeterminate; defenders see it as protecting democracy from itself.

Close with

Kesavananda refined Golaknath: it protected the Constitution's identity while letting the text evolve.

Previous-year questions (9)
  1. 2026Keshavananda illustrates judicial creativity and the policy-making role of the Supreme Court of a very high order and could be regarded to be an improvement over the formulation in Golaknath. Examine with the help of relevant arguments. [P-I 3(a), 20 marks]
    How to approach: Examine with arguments. Golaknath's rigid bar and prospective overruling → its problems → 24th Amendment → Kesavananda's flexible limit (amend but not destroy). Argue it is both creativity (judge-made doctrine) and improvement (allows reform). Present the counter-majoritarian critique too.
  2. 2025"It was claimed in the Constituent Assembly that the Constitution of India has, in fact, laid down a very 'facile' procedure for the amendment of the Constitution." Do you think the Doctrine of Basic Structure significantly limits the amending power under Article 368? Elucidate. [2c]
  3. 2024Discuss the procedure of amending the Constitution. Are there any restrictions also in this regard? Support your answer with the help of relevant Supreme Court judgments. [3c]
  4. 2022"Amending power does not extend to damaging or destroying the basic structure or framework of our Constitution." Discuss. [2a]
  5. 2020The goals specified in the Preamble contain the basic structure of the Constitution, which cannot be amended under Article 368 of the Constitution. Analyse this statement in the light of leading decided cases. [1a]
  6. 2018"Power of the Parliament to amend the Constitution is wide, but not unlimited." Do you agree with this statement? Discuss whether the doctrine of basic structure has reinforced the power of judicial review under the Constitution. [2b]
  7. 2016"The procedure adopted for amending the Constitution is unique; it is not rigid yet difficult." Elaborate. [1b]
  8. 2014Define and distinguish between 'Constituent power', 'Amending power' and 'Legislative power'. Give illustrations. [4a]
  9. 2013The goals specified in the Preamble contain the basic structure of our Constitution, which cannot be amended under Article 368. Elaborate in the context of leading cases. [3a]
