Why ADR

India has over five crore pending cases. Alternative dispute resolution (ADR) offers speed, lower cost, confidentiality, flexibility and preservation of relationships. Constitutional anchor: Art. 39A (equal justice and free legal aid).

Methods

  1. Negotiation: direct, without a third party.
  2. Mediation: a neutral facilitates a voluntary settlement; the mediator does not decide.
  3. Conciliation: like mediation, but the conciliator may propose terms (A&C Act, Part III).
  4. Arbitration: a private tribunal gives a binding award (A&C Act, 1996).
  5. Lok Adalats: settlement by conciliation; awards deemed decrees, with no appeal (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, ss. 19–22).
  6. Permanent Lok Adalats for public utility services (s. 22B onwards), which can decide on merits.
  7. Gram Nyayalayas (Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008): village-level courts using conciliation.
  8. Online dispute resolution (ODR): NITI Aayog's report (2021) and ODR provisions in the Mediation Act.

Court-annexed ADR

  • CPC s. 89 (inserted 1999, in force 2002): the court may refer disputes to arbitration, conciliation, judicial settlement including Lok Adalat, or mediation.
  • Salem Advocate Bar Association v. Union of India (2003, 2005): upheld s. 89 and approved model mediation rules.
  • Afcons Infrastructure v. Cherian Varkey Construction (2010): explained s. 89; listed cases suitable (commercial, matrimonial, consumer, tort, family) and unsuitable (representative suits, election petitions, serious fraud, criminal non-compoundable offences).
  • Commercial Courts Act, 2015, s. 12A: mandatory pre-institution mediation for commercial suits not seeking urgent interim relief. Patil Automation v. Rakheja Engineers (2022): s. 12A is mandatory; suits filed without it are liable to be rejected (prospectively from 20 August 2022).

The Mediation Act, 2023

  • Covers pre-litigation and court-annexed mediation, including online mediation and community mediation.
  • Mediated settlement agreements are final and binding and enforceable like court judgments; can be challenged only on limited grounds (fraud, corruption, impersonation, non-mediable disputes).
  • Time limit of 120 days (extendable by 60 days).
  • Mediation Council of India to register mediators and institutions.
  • Excludes disputes such as criminal prosecutions, rights of third parties, and matters listed in the First Schedule.
  • Provisions are being brought into force in phases.

International

  • Singapore Convention on Mediation (2019): India signed in 2019 but has not ratified.
  • New York Convention (1958) for arbitral awards; India's notified reciprocating territories.
  • India's aspiration to become an arbitration hub: India International Arbitration Centre (2019).

Assessment

  • Strengths: Lok Adalats dispose of crores of cases; mediation centres in courts; statutory backing.
  • Concerns: Lok Adalats often settle on unequal terms; a lack of trained mediators; the costs and delays of arbitration; enforcement and court interference.
  • Way forward: institutional arbitration, mediation training, ODR, public awareness, ratification of the Singapore Convention.

Revision points

  • Methods; Art. 39A; LSA Act Lok Adalats (award = decree, no appeal).
  • CPC s. 89; Salem Advocate (2005); Afcons (2010).
  • Commercial Courts Act s. 12A; Patil Automation (2022).
  • Mediation Act 2023 key features; Singapore Convention (signed, not ratified).
Syllabus unit 5 · Asked 5 times · 2014–2026

Answer writing: Alternate Dispute Resolution: Concept, types/prospects

Decode the demand. Questions test the types of ADR and their limits in public-law disputes.

Open with

ADR is not alternative to justice but alternative to adjudication; it suits disputes the parties can settle.

Answer frame

  1. Types: arbitration, mediation, conciliation, Lok Adalat
  2. Legal framework
  3. Advantages and data
  4. Limits: arbitrability, public law, constitutional rights
  5. Reform

Concept in brief

ADR covers arbitration, conciliation, mediation, negotiation and Lok Adalats. CPC s. 89 lets courts refer disputes; Lok Adalat awards are deemed decrees; pre-institution mediation is mandatory in commercial suits not seeking urgent relief. ADR suits private, contractual disputes, but disputes involving constitutional rights, crimes or public interest generally require adjudication by courts.

Key provisions

  • CPC s. 89, Order X rr. 1A–1C
  • Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, ss. 19–22
  • Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996
  • Mediation Act, 2023
  • Commercial Courts Act, 2015, s. 12A

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Salem Advocate Bar Assn. v. Union of India (2005): s. 89 framework.
  • Afcons Infrastructure v. Cherian Varkey Construction (2010): Which cases suit ADR.
  • Patil Automation v. Rakheja Engineers (2022): s. 12A mediation mandatory.
  • Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corp. (2021): Limits of arbitrability.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Mediation Act, 2023
  • Pendency of about five crore cases (NJDG)
  • Singapore Convention on Mediation (India signed 2019)

Critical lens

Rights-based and public-interest disputes need a public, reasoned decision.

Close with

ADR complements courts; it cannot replace them where rights against the State are at stake.

Previous-year questions (5)
  1. 2026Can ‘Alternative Dispute Resolution’ (ADR) mechanisms effectively adjudicate disputes involving public law elements, constitutional rights, or matters of significant public interest? Critically discuss the role of ADR mechanisms in light of limitations under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. [P-II 7(a), 20 marks]
    How to approach: Critically discuss. ADR suits private and contractual disputes; limits: arbitrability (Booz Allen, Vidya Drolia), public policy, rights in rem, criminal and constitutional matters; benefits for pendency. Balanced close.
  2. 2019"Alternative dispute resolution mechanism as provided under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 has played a pivotal role in dispensation of justice to the needy persons." Explain with the help of legal provisions and case law.
  3. 2018Even though Section 89 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, provides for out-of-court settlement of civil disputes filed in a civil court, the impact of such settlement through Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) appears to be poor. Analyse the reasons for failure to settle the disputes through ADR modes.
  4. 2015Discuss the various principles of 'mediation'.
  5. 2014"By and large, Lok-Adalats have failed in achieving the objects for which they were created." Comment. And also suggest some measures to make this institution more effective.
