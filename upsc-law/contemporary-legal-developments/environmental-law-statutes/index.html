Environmental law statutes

Syllabus unit 6 · Asked 14 times · 2013–2026

Syllabus: Major statutes concerning environmental law

Decode the demand. Questions test statutes, principles (precautionary, polluter pays, public trust) and institutions such as the NGT.

Open with

Indian environmental law combines statutes with judge-made principles rooted in Art. 21.

Answer frame

  1. Constitutional basis
  2. Key statutes
  3. Principles and their adoption in India
  4. Institutions: CPCB, NGT
  5. Gaps and reform

Concept in brief

India's environmental law rests on Arts. 21, 48A and 51A(g) and statutes on water, air, forests, wildlife, biodiversity and environment protection, with the NGT as a specialised forum. Courts have adopted sustainable development, the precautionary principle (replacing the older assimilative-capacity principle), polluter pays and public trust.

Key provisions

  • Water Act, 1974
  • Air Act, 1981
  • EPA, 1986
  • Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 (Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 2023)
  • Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972
  • Biological Diversity Act, 2002
  • NGT Act, 2010
  • Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996): Precaution and polluter pays.
  • A.P. Pollution Control Board v. M.V. Nayudu (1999): Shift from assimilative capacity to precaution; reverse burden.
  • M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997): Public trust doctrine.
  • T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1997): Dictionary meaning of 'forest'.
  • Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991): Pollution-free water and air in Art. 21.
  • M.K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (2024): Right against climate change effects.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • Shift from assimilative capacity (Stockholm) to precaution (Rio Principle 15), traced in Nayudu
  • Air-quality data and NCAP
  • Forest (Conservation) Amendment, 2023 debate

Critical lens

Strong principles but weak enforcement and frequent dilution of clearances.

Close with

Sustainable development must mean development within ecological limits.

Previous-year questions (14)
  1. 2026In international environmental jurisprudence, the emergence of the ‘precautionary principle’ marks a shift from the assimilative capacity principle to the precautionary principle. Elucidate this statement in light of the concept of ‘sustainable development’. [P-II 7(b), 15 marks]
    How to approach: Elucidate. Assimilative capacity (Stockholm) vs precaution (Rio Principle 15); reverse burden (Nayudu, Vellore); link to sustainable development (Brundtland, Gabčíkovo). Example: EIA and coastal regulation.
  2. 2025"The 'precautionary principle' and the 'polluter pays principle' are essential principles of sustainable development." Explain both the principles and their contribution to sustainable development, referring to relevant case laws.
  3. 2023What kind of cases are heard by the 'National-Green Tribunal'? How is it different from the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)?
  4. 2022Discuss the implications of the High Level Committee (known as T. S. R. Subramanian Committee) Report, 2014, for the review of environment-related laws in India.
  5. 2021India's 40-year-old 'Air Act', 1981, languishes in the present circumstances of the Air pollution emergency in Delhi - National Capital Region. Comment on the effectiveness of the law in the light of the judicial and administrative mechanisms.
  6. 2020Discuss the access and benefit sharing (ABS) law provided under the Biodiversity Conservation Act, 2002, with special reference to adjudication of biodiversity disputes and jurisdiction of the National Green Tribunal.
  7. 2020"The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 is an umbrella legislation to not only protect and improve the environment but also to prevent and control pollution." Comment and analyse.
  8. 2019"The National Green Tribunal, which was established for effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests and other natural resources, has played a pivotal role in the recent past in this regard." Examine the statement with reference to the pronouncements given by the National Green Tribunal.
  9. 2018'Sustainable development' has been accepted as a balancing concept between ecology and development. Discuss the recognition and application of this principle under the laws relating to environmental protection in India.
  10. 2017Discuss the constitution, jurisdiction, powers and authority of the National Green Tribunal. How far has it been successful in achieving its objectives? Explain with the help of recent cases.
  11. 2016"The object of the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 is to provide relief to the victims of accidents in hazardous industries in addition to any other right to claim compensation." Explain with case law.
  12. 2015Write a note on 'Impact of noise pollution on the health of the people.'
  13. 2014The doctrine of "Public Trust" as propounded by the Supreme Court has worked as an instrument for the protection of the Environment in India. Discuss.
  14. 2013Corporate causing havoc to the wildlife existing on seashores is an extreme type of environmental crime, and laws are almost non-existent to deal with them directly. Discuss.
