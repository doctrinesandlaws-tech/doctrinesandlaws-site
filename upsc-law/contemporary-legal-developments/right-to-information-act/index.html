Right to Information Act, 2005

Syllabus: Right to Information Act

Decode the demand. Questions test RTI's objectives, exemptions and recent amendments.

Open with

RTI turned the citizen from a supplicant into a rights-holder against the State.

Answer frame

  1. Constitutional basis and objectives
  2. Key provisions and timelines
  3. Exemptions and public interest
  4. Amendments (2019, DPDP 2023)
  5. Assessment

Concept in brief

The RTI Act, 2005 operationalises the right to know under Art. 19(1)(a). Public authorities must disclose proactively (s. 4) and answer requests within 30 days, subject to exemptions (s. 8) and severability. The 2019 amendment let the Centre fix commissioners' tenure and pay; the DPDP Act, 2023 amended s. 8(1)(j) to exempt personal information outright.

Key provisions

  • RTI Act ss. 2(f), 2(h), 2(j), 4, 6–8, 11, 19, 20, 24

Leading cases (cite the ratio, not the facts)

  • State of U.P. v. Raj Narain (1975): Right to know.
  • CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay (2011): Evaluated answer sheets are information.
  • Girish Ramchandra Deshpande v. CIC (2012): Service records are personal information.
  • Thalappalam Service Co-op Bank v. State of Kerala (2013): Meaning of 'public authority'.
  • CPIO, Supreme Court v. Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019): Office of the CJI is a public authority.
  • Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2024): Electoral bond scheme struck down for violating the right to information.

Interdisciplinary edge

  • 2nd ARC 1st Report on the right to information as the 'master key' to good governance
  • MKSS movement in Rajasthan
  • Data: pending appeals before Information Commissions

Critical lens

Vacancies and the 2023 change to s. 8(1)(j) weaken transparency.

Close with

An independent and adequately staffed Information Commission is the backbone of RTI.

Previous-year questions (10)
  1. 2025"Right to Information, for citizens to secure access to information under the control of public authorities, is an important enactment." Discuss. Also, describe the obligations of public authorities as mentioned in the Act.
  2. 2024An information shall ordinarily be provided in the form in which it is sought. Are there any exceptions to this rule? Explain with suitable illustrations.
  3. 2023Describe the constitutional roots of the 'Right to Information' in India. Refer to the decided case laws.
  4. 2021Discuss the constitutionality of the Right to Information Act, 2019, in the light of the recent judgment by the Supreme Court of India.
  5. 2020Discuss the rationale of exemption to disclosure of information endangering life and the information regarding criminal trial and criminal investigation provided under the Right to Information Act, 2005, in the light of decided cases.
  6. 2019"Pragmatic regime of right to information for citizens is the key to good governance in India, but it is not being implemented in its original spirit." Examine it in the light of the decision of the Supreme Court of India in Anjali Bhardwaj vs. Union of India, February 2019.
  7. 2018"Mahatma Gandhi, the Father of the Nation, observed that the meaning of real freedom is not to acquire authority by a few but to acquire the capacity to question the abuse of such authority." Examine, in the light of the above statement, the obligations of the public authorities and explain whether they have discharged it effectively during the last about seven decades.
  8. 2017"Notwithstanding transparency of governance, certain information has been exempted from disclosure under the Right to Information Act, 2005." Discuss the relevant provisions and limitations on disclosure of information.
  9. 2016"The Right to Information Act, 2005 was enacted in order to promote transparency and accountability in the working of every public authority." How far has this goal been achieved by the Right to Information Act, 2005, in the last ten years? Critically analyse your answer with the support of exceptions and case law.
  10. 2013“An attempt by all political parties to bring amendments to the RTI Act, 2005, is to sabotage the steps towards transparency of governance in this country.” Critically evaluate the statement.
